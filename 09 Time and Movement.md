# Chapter 9: Time and Movement

## Time

Time governs every activity in the campaign world. Travel, exploration, spellcasting, combat, rest, research, construction, and recovery all consume time, sometimes in moments and sometimes in months. While real time passes for the players at the table, game time passes for the characters within the world, and the two are not necessarily related.

Campaign time is normally measured using familiar units such as days, weeks, months, and years, though individual settings may employ entirely different calendars and systems of reckoning.

Several shorter units of time are used frequently in play:

- A **round** represents approximately one minute of action.
    
- A **turn** equals 10 minutes.
    
- A **day of travel** normally assumes roughly 10 hours of movement, excluding extended rests.
    
Some rules also refer to **segments**. Segments are not fixed units of measurable time, but abstract divisions used to determine relative order during fast-moving events, especially combat and spellcasting. Their exact duration is intentionally imprecise and should not be treated as literal seconds.

## Movement

Every creature possesses a base movement rate reflecting its normal overland walking pace under favourable conditions.

**Table 52: Base Movement Rates**
 
|Race|Rate|
|---|---|
|Human|12|
|Dwarf|6|
|Elf|12|
|Half-elf|12|
|Gnome|6|
|Halfling|6|

A creature normally moves a number of **tens of yards per round** equal to its movement rate during overland or outdoor movement. Thus, a human with Movement 12 normally travels 120 yards per round.

In confined, hazardous, or carefully explored environments such as dungeons, caves, ruins, or similar settings, movement is reduced to **tens of feet per round**. This slower pace reflects caution, observation, mapping, stealth, and the need to avoid traps or ambushes.

Encumbrance may reduce movement rates substantially.

#### Rapid Movement

Characters may move more quickly at the expense of caution.

When moving at full overland speed within confined environments, characters suffer a –1 penalty to surprise rolls while granting opponents a +1 bonus to surprise them. Rapid movement prevents effective searching and normally precludes noticing traps, concealed doors, subtle hazards, or similar details.

Combat movement, running, sprinting, and swimming movement are governed by the rules established in the Combat chapter.

#### Overland Travel

Under normal conditions, a creature may travel a number of miles per day equal to twice its movement rate. A human with Movement 12 therefore travels approximately 24 miles during a normal marching day.

Terrain, weather, roads, visibility, mounts, supply conditions, exhaustion, and encumbrance may substantially alter travel rates.

#### Forced Marches

A forced march increases daily travel to 2½ times movement rate in miles per day. At the end of each day of forced travel, characters must succeed at a Constitution check or become fatigued.

Each consecutive day of forced marching applies a cumulative –1 penalty to the Constitution check and inflicts a cumulative –1 penalty to attack rolls, proficiency checks, and similar strenuous activities due to exhaustion. Half a full day of rest removes one day of accumulated penalties.

Failure prevents further forced marching until sufficient rest has been obtained, though ordinary travel may continue.

### Swimming

Swimming ability varies greatly between individuals, cultures, and races. Characters may be either untrained swimmers or proficient swimmers, at DM discretion or according to proficiency selection.

Characters wearing metal armour, carrying excessive encumbrance, or reduced to one-third or less of normal movement generally cannot swim effectively and risk immediate sinking.

#### Swimming Rates

A proficient swimmer may normally move at one-half current land movement rate in yards per round while swimming. Thus, a creature with Movement 12 swims 60 yards per round.

A successful Strength check may increase swimming speed, as described in the Combat chapter.

#### Swimming Fatigue

Swimming for extended periods is exhausting.

A character swimming at ordinary pace or merely staying afloat may continue for a number of hours equal to Constitution before checks become necessary. After this point, a Constitution check is required each additional hour. Failure forces the swimmer to rest, tread water, or risk drowning at DM discretion.

Rough water, storms, strong currents, cold conditions, surf, or dangerous seas may require much more frequent checks.

Long-duration swimming commonly inflicts cumulative fatigue penalties to attack rolls and physical activities. Particularly severe conditions may temporarily reduce Strength or Constitution until adequate rest is obtained.

#### Holding Breath

Under ordinary conditions, a character may hold his breath for a number of rounds equal to one-third Constitution, rounded up. Strenuous activity halves this duration.

After this limit is exceeded, the character must make a Constitution check each round with a cumulative –2 penalty per additional round. Failure means the character must breathe or begin drowning.

#### Diving and Surfacing

Characters may normally dive or ascend approximately 20 feet per round while swimming freely. Encumbrance, armour, currents, injuries, poor visibility, or adverse conditions may significantly reduce this rate.

Weighted or heavily encumbered creatures may sink uncontrollably and become unable to resurface without assistance.

### Climbing

All characters possess some limited ability to climb, though training and equipment greatly improve success.

Three broad climbing categories are recognized:

- **Thieves** possess specialized climbing ability.
    
- **Mountaineers** possess formal training or proficiency.
    
- **Unskilled climbers** rely entirely upon ordinary physical ability.

#### Climbing Rates

Climbing movement depends upon surface type, weather, equipment, and climbing skill.

**Table 55: Rates of Climbing**
   
|Surface Type|Dry|Slightly Slippery|Slippery|
|---|---|---|---|
|Very smooth|¼|—|—|
|Smooth and cracked|½|⅓|¼|
|Rough|1|⅓|¼|
|Rough with ledges|1|½|⅓|
|Ice wall|—|—|¼|
|Tree or scaffold|4|3|2|
|Sloping wall|3|2|1|
|Rope and wall|2|1|½|

Multiply the listed value by current movement rate to determine feet climbed per round. Thieves climb at double these listed rates.

Very smooth, smooth, or rough cliff faces generally require mountaineering equipment for non-thieves.

#### Climbing Hazards

Characters climbing lose Dexterity bonuses to Armour Class and are generally treated as vulnerable to rear attacks. Attacks, saving throws, and proficiency checks commonly suffer a –2 penalty while actively climbing.

Characters struck while climbing must immediately succeed at another climbing check or lose position. Unsecured climbers who fail may fall.

Two-handed weapons cannot normally be used while climbing.

#### Climbing Equipment

Ropes, pitons, harnesses, spikes, grapnels, and similar tools primarily reduce the danger of falling rather than improving climbing speed.

Properly secured ropes may arrest falls entirely or greatly reduce falling distance. Pitons, anchors, and belaying systems may fail under sudden stress at DM discretion.

Rappelling permits rapid descent using secured rope systems. Characters may normally rappel at ordinary dungeon movement rate. Failed climbing checks during rappelling may result in dangerous falls or uncontrolled descent.
