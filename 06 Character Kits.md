# Chapter 6:Character Kits

Character kits are a tool to help players develop their characters into a specific role, archetype, or identity within the game world. The DM may restrict which kits are available in the campaign and may create additional kits tailored to specific factions, orders, or cultures in their world.

For most characters, choosing a kit is optional; there is no disadvantage to choosing to play a generic fighter, thief, or other class. The exception is when a kit allows a character to belong to a class for which they would otherwise be ineligible.

A character may have only one character kit. In general, this kit must be chosen at character creation, or, for characters who choose to dual-class, when they gain their new class. Kits associated with membership in a specific organization may instead be chosen when the character joins that organization. Such a kit replaces any kit the character already possesses. Required kits may not be replaced.

Some kits may be abandoned, in which case the character loses all of the kit's benefits and hindrances and reverts to their base class or classes. Other kits—typically those representing a character's origins, as well as required kits—cannot be abandoned. In some cases, abandoning a kit may be as simple as making the decision to do so; in others, it may require the character to undertake specific actions or fulfill particular conditions. The DM determines whether a kit can be abandoned and what, if anything, is required to do so.

### Composition of Character Kits

Each character kit consists of recommendations, bonuses and benefits which are offset by a number of requirements, obligations. and restrictions. Kits are presented as follows, with any non-relevant sections omitted:

Requirements – abilities, etc.

Description – flavourful description

Role – how they fit in party/campaign/world

Weapon Proficiencies – required, recommended, barred

Nonweapon Proficiencies – bonus, recommended, barred

Wealth – starting gold, limits on (such as tithes)

Equipment – requirements and restrictions

Rogue Skills -

Magic – preferred and/or barred schools/spheres

Benefits – what the kit grants outside of the other categories

Restrictions – the cost offsetting the benefits

Additional Notes – racial variations of a kit, etc.

## Character Kit Descriptions

### Warrior Kits

#### Amazon Warrior

**Requirements:**

Class: Fighter or Ranger;

Other: Female gender.

**Description:** Amazons are women who come from societies in which women traditionally occupy the roles of warriors, leaders, and heads of households. Amazon societies vary considerably. Some are isolated communities with their own customs and traditions; others are established kingdoms or city-states that have maintained their independence for generations. Their origins are equally varied. Some trace their traditions to a deity or legendary founder, while others arose from historical migrations, political revolutions, or simply developed along different lines from neighbouring societies.

There is no single Amazon culture. Some are highly organized and sophisticated, with extensive trade and diplomatic relationships; others are small and remote. Their attitudes toward men also vary. In some communities men still occupy some important roles in society, while in others social and political authority is exclusively held by women. What they share is the expectation that women are fully capable of taking responsibility for the defence of their communities.

Amazon warriors are commonly trained from an early age in mounted combat, hunting, and the use of spear and bow. The equipment favoured by a particular community reflects its environment and history. Most Amazon cultures favour relatively light armour and mobile fighting styles, but an Amazon who leaves her homeland is not bound to those traditions.

**Role:** The Amazon is a warrior trained to defend her community and accustomed to taking responsibility for its safety. She is typically a mobile combatant, skilled in mounted warfare, archery, and the spear. Her training also makes her well suited to hunting, tracking, and wilderness travel.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** Required: Spear, Long Bow; Barred: Specialization in any weapon other than Spear or Long Bow; Recommended: Axe, Sword.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Riding (Land-Based), Animal Training; Recommended: Animal Handling, Animal Lore, Armourer, Bowyer/Fletcher, Hunting, Running, Survival, Tracking.

**Equipment:** At character creation, an Amazon may purchase only the following weapons and armour: Battle Axe, Bow (Any), Club, Dagger/Dirk, Hand Axe, Throwing Axe, Javelin, Knife, Lance, Spear, Sword (any); Shield, Leather, Padded, Studded Leather, Brigandine, Scale Mail, Hide, Banded Mail, Bronze Plate Mail. Once she has gained sufficient experience of other cultures through adventuring, she may purchase and use weapons and armour customary to those cultures.

**Benefits:** When an Amazon first fights an opponent from a male-dominated society, that opponent may underestimate her because of her sex and unfamiliarity with women trained as warriors. Against such an opponent, the Amazon gains a +3 bonus to attack rolls until her first successful attack. and the first successful attack also gains a +3 bonus to the damage roll.

Warriors of 5th level or higher, other characters of 8th level or higher, and any characters who has witnessed the amazon using this tactic automatically recognize that the Amazon is a trained warrior and will not underestimate her.

**Restrictions:** An Amazon suffers a −3 penalty to reaction rolls when dealing with NPCs from male-dominated societies who are unfamiliar with her. This penalty does not apply to NPCs who have come to respect her or who are already familiar with Amazon warriors.

**Additional Notes:** .  
**Dwarven Amazons:** Replace the required Spear and Long Bow proficiencies with Axe and Hammer. Dwarven Amazons traditionally favour the boar as a mount and gain the same benefits from Riding (Land-Based) when riding a trained boar.

**Gnome Amazons:** Replace the required Spear and Long Bow proficiencies with Throwing Axe and Short Sword. Replace the bonus Riding (Land-Based) and Animal Training proficiencies with Tracking and Survival.

**Halfling Amazons:** Replace the required Spear and Long Bow proficiencies with Javelin and Sling. Replace the bonus Riding (Land-Based) and Animal Training proficiencies with Endurance and Set Snares.

#### Berserker

**Requirements:**

Ability: Strength 15+;

Class: Fighter, Barbarian.

**Description:** The Berserker is a warrior capable of entering a state of supernatural combat fury. When this state takes hold, he can fight with extraordinary endurance, disregard for pain, and ferocity. The Berserker's fury may arise from intense discipline, a spiritual tradition, an ancestral or supernatural influence, or an instinctive aspect of his nature.

The Berserker need not be a primitive warrior or belong to a tribal culture. He may be a member of a warrior society, a soldier trained in a particular battle tradition, a champion associated with a supernatural power, or an otherwise ordinary warrior who possesses an extraordinary capacity for battle-fury.

**Role:** The Berserker is a formidable close-combat warrior whose greatest strength comes with considerable risk. His abilities make him exceptionally effective when committed to a difficult fight, but his loss of control can endanger himself and his allies.

A Berserker's fury may be regarded as a gift, a sacred duty, a feared affliction, or simply another weapon. The source and cultural interpretation of the ability should be established when the character is created.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** The Berserker may not begin play proficient with a ranged weapon, including thrown weapons. He may learn ranged weapons after play begins.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Endurance; Recommended: Animal Handling, Animal Training, Direction Sense, Fire-Building, Riding (Land-Based), Weather Sense, Blind-Fighting, Hunting, Mountaineering, Running, Set Snares, Survival, Tracking, Herbalism, Jumping. The DM may require a proficiency appropriate to the character's culture or warrior tradition.

**Equipment:** The Berserker must spend all but 3 gp before play begins. His initial armour may not be heavier than splint mail, banded mail, or bronze plate mail. His starting weapons must be appropriate to the character's culture or warrior tradition and may not include ranged weapons.

**Benefits:** The Berserker can enter a state of supernatural combat fury known as **Berserk**.

The Berserker must spend time working himself into a frenzy before entering this state. This preparation is conspicuous and cannot be performed quietly. It requires 1 turn (10 rounds) but does not prevent other actions – the character may still fight or use skills as normal, though lengthy dialogue might be difficult while growling and grunting.

A character may begin preparing to berserk before combat. This still requires 10 minutes (1 turn) and once prepared he can immediately begin the berserk at any time in the following 50 minutes (5 turns). If combat does not begin within this time, the Berserker automatically returns to his normal state and suffers the usual consequences of ending a Berserk.

Once in the berserk state the character remains in that state as long as there as an enemy capable of fighting is in sight. and must leave the state when the combat ends.

While Berserk, the character gains the following benefits:

- **Combat Fury:** +1 to attack rolls, +3 to damage rolls, and +5 temporary hit points.
    
- **Mental Resistance:** The Berserker is immune to effects that would magically control, pacify, weaken, or frighten him as specified by the Berserk rules. Where an effect is not negated outright, he receives a +4 bonus to the applicable saving throw.
    
- **Pain Resistance:** The Berserker is immune to knockout results from Punching and Wrestling and takes half damage from bare-handed attacks using those rules.
    
- **Death Magic:** Effects that would kill or inflict delayed damage upon the Berserker may be suspended until his Berserk ends, as specified under the consequences of ending Berserk.
    

The exact interaction between Berserk and individual spells or magical effects is governed by the Berserk rules in the Magic chapter.

**Restrictions:** The Berserker suffers a –3 reaction adjustment from NPCs who are unfamiliar with his condition or tradition. Those accustomed to Berserkers may instead recognize and respect him.

While Berserk:

- The Berserker may not use ranged weapons.
    
- He must fight opponents in melee and must continue fighting until his current opponent is defeated before moving to another enemy.
    
- When an opponent falls, he must attack the nearest available enemy rather than selecting a distant or strategically important target.
    
- He may not deliberately take cover from missile fire.
    
- If another character appears to attack or obstruct him, he must test against Intelligence to determine whether he recognizes the character as an ally. On a successful test, he recognizes that the character is not attacking him; on a failure, he treats the character as an enemy until the Berserk ends.
    
- _Taunt_ automatically affects him and causes him to abandon his current opponent and attack the taunting character.
    
- Beneficial healing and restorative magic does not affect him until his Berserk ends.
    

The Berserker cannot voluntarily end his Berserk while enemies remain capable of fighting. Once the last enemy is down, he may end the state and must do so when the encounter is over.

When Berserk ends, the Berserker loses the 5 temporary hit points gained from the ability. He then collapses from exhaustion, suffering a –4 penalty to Strength for a number of rounds equal to the number of rounds he spent Berserk. Any damage or magical effects delayed by Berserk take effect at this time, after which healing magic may affect him normally.

If the loss of the temporary hit points reduces the Berserker to 0 hit points or below, the normal rules for death and dying apply.

**Additional Notes:** The Berserker need not belong to a culture traditionally associated with berserk warriors. His ability may instead arise from a family tradition, religious practice, supernatural patron, martial discipline, or other source appropriate to the campaign.

Dwarves are rarely Berserkers, and more often follow the dwarven tradition of the Battlerager.

#### Cavalier

**Requirements:**

Abilities: Strength 15+, Dexterity 15+, Constitution 15+, Intelligence 10+, Wisdom 10+;

Class: Fighter or Paladin

Alignment: Any Good;

Race: Human, Elf, Half-Elf

**Description:** The Cavalier is a mounted warrior of a chivalric culture, trained to serve as both a fighting man and a representative of the noble class. Cavaliers are typically drawn from the hereditary nobility, though an impoverished noble family may possess little wealth beyond its name, traditions, and obligations.

The Cavalier's ideals centre on courage, personal honour, service, and martial excellence. A Cavalier may serve a lord, defend a community, undertake quests on behalf of others, or seek personal renown through worthy deeds. The precise customs of chivalry vary between cultures, and the Cavalier need not resemble the knight of any particular historical tradition. What distinguishes the Cavalier is the combination of noble status, martial training, and a strong personal code.

**Role:** The Cavalier is a front-line warrior and mounted combatant. He is expected to lead by example, accept dangerous assignments, defend those placed in his care, and represent his lord or culture honourably. His social position also gives him responsibilities and privileges that extend beyond the battlefield.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** Required: Lance (any), Sword (any); Recommended: Other Lances, Other Swords, Horseman's weapons, Dagger, Spear, Javelin.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Riding (Land-Based), Etiquette; Recommended: Animal Handling, Animal Training, Dancing, Heraldry, Musical Instrument, Reading/Writing, Blind-Fighting, Endurance.

**Equipment:** With his starting gold, the Cavalier must purchase one lance, one sword, and the most expensive armour he can afford. Any remaining funds may be spent normally. The cavalier also begins play with a war horse of his choice - light, medium, or heavy - and associated war saddle, saddle blanket, and bridoon. If it dies the cavalier must obtain another war horse of charger quality and will not willingly accept an inferior mount.

**Benefits:** At 1st level, the Cavalier gains a +1 attack bonus with lances when used from horseback. This bonus increases by +1 at 7th level and every six levels thereafter. At 3rd level, he gains a +1 attack bonus with one type of sword of his choice; this increases by +1 at 9th level and every six levels thereafter. At 5th level, he gains a +1 attack bonus with one of horseman's mace, flail, or pick; this increases by +1 at 11th level and every six levels thereafter. These bonuses do not apply to damage and do not allow the Cavalier to strike creatures that require magical weapons to hit.

The Cavalier is immune to magical fear. While fighting, he radiates an aura of courage in a 10-foot radius that negates the effects of fear, but does not provide the other benefits of the _emotion_ spell.

The Cavalier gains a +4 bonus to saving throws against magic that would affect his mind.

The Cavalier gains a +3 reaction adjustment from members of his own culture, except criminals and characters of Evil alignment, who instead suffer a −3 reaction adjustment toward him.

A Cavalier may demand shelter from non-nobles when travelling within his own nation. Nobles and others of comparable status will generally provide hospitality willingly.

**Restrictions:** The Cavalier cannot attack an opponent at range if it is reasonably possible to charge or engage that opponent in melee. He must meet worthy enemies directly whenever circumstances permit. This restriction does not prevent him from using a ranged attack when doing so is necessary to protect an innocent person or prevent an immediate greater danger.

When choosing a target in combat, the Cavalier must favour the most powerful or dangerous opponent available. He may deal with lesser opponents who prevent him from reaching that foe, but should not deliberately avoid the principal threat in favour of weaker targets.

The Cavalier must maintain the highest-quality armour he can reasonably afford. When sufficient wealth becomes available, he is expected to upgrade to better armour, ultimately seeking full plate. The quality of the armour is more important to him than magical bonuses; he prefers ordinary field plate to inferior armour with a large magical bonus.

The Cavalier must observe a Code of Chivalry appropriate to his culture. The code governs his conduct and obligations, but does not require a particular alignment. At minimum, it requires him to accept worthy quests and noble service, defend those placed in his charge, show courage in the service of his cause, fulfill his obligations to those he has sworn to serve, conduct himself with courtesy, pursue honour through worthy deeds, and accept death rather than knowingly commit an act that would bring dishonour upon himself or those he serves.

A first deliberate violation of the code results in a warning from the DM. A second violation causes the Cavalier to lose all benefits of this kit until he repents and successfully completes a dangerous task of atonement while observing his code. A third violation constitutes abandonment of the kit. The Cavalier permanently loses its benefits, no longer follows its code, and suffers a −3 reaction adjustment from members of his own culture. His war horse will no longer accept him as a rider.

**Additional Notes:** The precise details of the Cavalier’s code, equipment, and social obligations may vary between cultures and should be discussed with the DM.

#### Gladiator

**Requirements:**

Class: Fighter (others at DM discretion)

**Description:** The Gladiator is a professional warrior from a society where public combat is a popular form of entertainment. Gladiators fight organized matches before crowds, for prize money, reputation, political influence, or the profits of their owners. Their opponents may be other warriors, criminals, slaves, monsters, or other unusual combatants, depending on the traditions of the arena.

Gladiatorial traditions vary considerably. Some emphasize lethal contests between heavily armed warriors; others favour elaborate displays of skill, unusual weapons, mounted or chariot combat, or contests in which the audience decides the outcome. Some gladiators are slaves, while others are free professionals who have entered the arena voluntarily. A gladiator may be a brutal killer, a disciplined athlete, a reluctant slave, or a celebrated public hero.

**Role:** The Gladiator is a versatile combatant accustomed to fighting in public and adapting quickly to unfamiliar opponents and circumstances. Gladiators are trained to make effective use of both conventional and unusual weapons and are often skilled at movement, spectacle, and fighting from unusual positions. A Gladiator may remain an active arena fighter while adventuring, or may have left the arena behind.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** Required: Short Sword or Gladius, Trident, Net; Recommended: A mixture of conventional and unusual weapons, including Bow, Cestus, Dagger, Drusus, Lasso, Scimitar, Spear, and Whip.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Charioteering, Tumbling; Recommended: Animal Handling, Animal Training, Armorer, Blind-Fighting, Endurance, Etiquette, Gaming, Healing, Riding (Land-Based), Weaponsmithing.

**Equipment:** A gladiators armour must be selected from the Gladiator Armor table.

**Benefits:** The Gladiator gains Proficiency _and_ Specialization in one additional weapon at character creation. This must be selected from Bow(any), Cestus, Dagger, Drusus, Gladius, Lasso, Net, Scimitar, Short Sword, Spear, Trident, or Whip.

**Restrictions:** Gladiators are recognizable as such in many places. Their appearance, manner, or reputation may make it difficult to conceal their identity or movements. NPCs who recognize the character may be able to identify him from descriptions given by witnesses.

Gladiators may also attract the attention of, or be actively sought out by, unscrupulous arena promoters, managers, sponsors, or former employers. Such individuals may attempt to coerce the character into returning to the arena, participating in particular contests, or undertaking other work connected with gladiatorial combat. The DM should ensure that such complications remain a common annoyance and occasional threat, rather than allowing them to dictate the character's actions or unduly restrict player agency.

#### Myrmidon

**Requirements:**

Ability: Strength 12+, Constitution 12+;

Class: Fighter, Ranger, Paladin.

**Description:** The Myrmidon is a veteran of organized military service. Soldiering was once his profession, whether he served as a common soldier, non-commissioned officer, officer, bodyguard, or mercenary. He has since left active service and taken up another life, but the habits, training, relationships, and reputation acquired during his military career remain part of who he is.

A Myrmidon may have served in a national army, city or palace guard, mercenary company, private military household, or other organized force. His service may have ended through retirement, injury, completion of a contract, political circumstances, or simple desire for a different life. He may still maintain ties to his former comrades and commanders, and in some circumstances may be expected or required to return to service.

A Myrmidon's former rank remains part of his identity. A retired captain may still be addressed as Captain by former comrades and others who recognize his service, while a veteran soldier may continue to be known by the rank he held when he left the army. Such recognition may bring privileges and respect in some places, but it can also create expectations.

**Role:** The Myrmidon is a disciplined and experienced warrior accustomed to operating as part of an organized force. He brings practical military knowledge to an adventuring party, including experience with tactics, fieldcraft, logistics, and the realities of military life. Although no longer an active soldier, he is accustomed to discipline and cooperation and may naturally assume a leadership role when circumstances demand it.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** Any.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Ancient History (Military History), Fire-Building; Recommended: Animal Handling, Armorer, Blind-Fighting, Bowyer/Fletcher, Charioteering, Cooking, Endurance, Heraldry, Navigation, Reading/Writing, Riding (Land-Based), Seamanship, Set Snares, Survival, Swimming, Tracking, Weaponsmithing, Weather Sense, Disguise.

**Equipment:** The Myrmidon may spend his starting wealth normally. He may retain military equipment from his former service if appropriate to his background and the campaign.

**Benefits:** The Myrmidon gains Proficiency and Specialization in one additional weapon at character creation. This must be selected from Battle Axe, Bow (Composite Long Bow, Composite Short Bow, or Long Bow), Crossbow (Heavy or Light), Lance (any), Polearm (any), Spear, or Sword (any).

The Myrmidon's former rank and service are recognized by the military organization in which he served and, where appropriate, by other members of the military profession. This may grant him privileges appropriate to his former rank, such as military hospitality, access to restricted areas, recognition by former comrades, or discounts and other courtesies customarily extended to veterans. The precise benefits depend upon the character's former service and the culture of the campaign.

**Restrictions:** The Myrmidon's military bearing makes him distinctive and easily recognizable. His posture, mannerisms, equipment, and disciplined conduct may cause witnesses to remember and describe him more readily than an otherwise inconspicuous character.

The Myrmidon remains subject to certain obligations arising from his former service. In particular, the military organization in which he served may call upon him in times of war or other exceptional need. The character is not expected to abandon his adventuring career whenever a former superior gives an ordinary order, but a genuine military emergency may create a serious obligation to return to service. The nature and extent of this obligation depend upon the terms under which the character left the service and the laws and customs of the organization.

**Additional Notes:** The Myrmidon's former military affiliation, rank, and circumstances of departure should be established when the character is created. He may have retired honourably, completed a contract, resigned his commission, been discharged, or left the service under less favourable circumstances. These circumstances should influence how former comrades, military authorities, and the wider society regard him.

#### Noble Warrior

**Requirements:**

Ability: Strength 13+, Constitution 13+;

Class: Fighter, Ranger, Paladin.

**Description:** The Noble Warrior is a member of the nobility, trained and expected to embody the ideals of his social class. In a traditional medieval-fantasy setting, these ideals include courage, chivalry, courtesy, protection of those who genuinely require it, and loyalty to the established social and political order. Noble Warriors are commonly called Knights or Squires, although the title and traditions may differ considerably between cultures and campaign settings.

The Noble Warrior need not be a European-style knight. He may instead belong to a warrior aristocracy, hereditary military caste, ruling clan, mounted nobility, or similar elite appropriate to the campaign.

**Role:** The Noble Warrior is a martial representative of the ruling class and is expected to be courageous, gallant, honourable, and protective of those under his authority. He may be a young knight seeking to prove himself, a squire serving an established lord, a noble cavalryman, or a veteran aristocratic warrior.

These are ideals rather than requirements of character. A Noble Warrior may sincerely uphold them, struggle to live up to them, or openly abuse the privileges of his station. His alignment and conduct determine what sort of noble he becomes.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** Required: Long Sword or Bastard Sword, Lance (any), Horseman's Flail or Horseman's Mace; Recommended: Weapons and fighting styles appropriate to mounted combat.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Etiquette, Heraldry, Riding (Land-Based); Recommended: Animal Training, Blind-Fighting, Dancing, Gaming, Hunting, Tracking, Local History (Priest, 2 slots unless Paladin), Musical Instrument, Reading/Writing.

**Equipment:** The Noble Warrior must begin play with at least brigandine or scale mail armour, a shield, one weapon larger than a dagger, and a horse. The horse must be at least a riding horse. He must also purchase a riding saddle, bit and bridle, horseshoes and shoeing, halter, and saddle blanket before play begins.

The Noble Warrior receives an additional 225 gp in starting funds, in addition to the normal starting funds for his class group. This additional wealth is intended in part to meet the kit's mandatory equipment requirements.

**Benefits:** The Noble Warrior receives a +3 reaction adjustment when dealing with members of his own culture.

When travelling within his own nation, the Noble Warrior may demand shelter from persons of lower social status. Nobles and others of equal or higher status will generally offer shelter when practical. A host of appropriate status will normally provide accommodation for the Noble Warrior and up to two companions per experience level, although the quality and extent of such hospitality depend upon the host and circumstances.

Within his own land, a Noble Warrior may exercise whatever customary authority his rank grants over commoners. In a feudal or similarly structured society, this may include judging minor offences, imposing customary penalties, or ordering the apprehension of offenders. The precise extent of this authority depends upon the laws and customs of the campaign.

**Restrictions:** The Noble Warrior has sworn an oath of loyalty to a superior noble, knight, ruler, or other authority appropriate to his rank. He is expected to honour this oath when called upon to do so. Depending on the circumstances, this may require military service, providing troops or resources, accompanying his lord on campaign, or supporting his lord at considerable personal expense. The oath does not normally require the character to abandon his adventuring career in response to every ordinary request, but genuine emergencies or obligations of exceptional importance may demand his service.

The Noble Warrior is also expected to maintain the standard of living appropriate to his rank. When purchasing goods, equipment, or services for himself or his household, he must normally spend **10% more than the listed cost for each experience level he has attained**, representing superior quality, craftsmanship, appearance, service, and other requirements of his station. This is not simply a gratuity or surcharge; the character receives a better-quality version of the goods or services purchased.

If the Noble Warrior cannot afford these higher standards, he may purchase ordinary goods instead. Each such occasion reduces his reaction adjustment from this kit by 1, to a minimum of +0, until he has restored his customary standard of living. The DM may also introduce appropriate social consequences, such as damaged reputation, reduced hospitality, or being regarded as a noble who has fallen on hard times.

Once the Noble Warrior has sufficient wealth to restore his position, he may spend whatever is necessary to replace inferior possessions and re-establish an appropriate standard of living. His full +3 reaction adjustment then returns.

A Noble Warrior who acquires a particularly bad reputation loses the benefits of his noble standing among those who know of it and instead suffers a –6 reaction adjustment from such individuals. The DM should apply this only when the character's reputation is genuinely known and relevant.

Noble hospitality is reciprocal. A Noble Warrior is expected to provide appropriate shelter, food, and assistance to nobles and other persons of comparable status who travel through his territory or encounter him while travelling. Such obligations may become expensive, particularly when several noble guests arrive unexpectedly.

**Additional Notes:** The precise rank, title, oath, hereditary status, and political privileges of a Noble Warrior should be established according to the campaign. A character may begin play as a newly knighted warrior, a squire still in service to another knight, or the equivalent rank within a different cultural tradition.

The Noble Warrior's social privileges should not be assumed to function outside the culture and political jurisdiction that recognizes his rank. Likewise, a Noble Warrior who belongs to a culture without hereditary nobility may instead represent membership in whatever warrior aristocracy or ruling elite fulfills an equivalent social role.

Any race may produce Noble Warriors where the campaign's culture and social institutions permit it.

#### Peasant Hero

**Description:** The Peasant Hero is the local warrior who rose from humble origins and became an adventurer to the admiration of the people of his home community. He may be a farmhand, hunter, labourer, villager, or the child of a rural family who took up the sword and left home to seek his fortune.

A Peasant Hero may be a rebel against an oppressive ruler, a farmboy destined to become a great general, or even a child of noble birth secretly raised among commoners. Whatever his origins or eventual achievements, he remembers his roots and remains connected to his family and home community.

**Role:** The Peasant Hero is a champion of ordinary people. He understands rural life and the concerns of those who work the land, and may be motivated by a desire to protect his family, improve conditions in his homeland, or simply prove that greatness is not limited to the privileged.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** The player may choose his character's weapon proficiencies, subject to the weapons normally available to peasants in the campaign's culture. Common choices include Short Sword, Spear, Bow, and other weapons suitable for foot soldiers, hunters, and villagers. Weapons normally associated with cavalry, elite warriors, or exotic foreign traditions may not be selected at character creation unless they are reasonably available to peasants in the campaign. This restriction applies only when the character is created; the Peasant Hero may subsequently learn any weapon for which he receives training.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Agriculture or Fishing; and Weather Sense or Animal Lore; Recommended: Any General proficiency.

**Equipment:** The Peasant Hero may spend his starting wealth as he chooses, but may have no more than 3 gp remaining when play begins.

**Benefits:** The Peasant Hero is welcomed in his home community and generally receives shelter and practical assistance from its people. Unless it is known that he has deliberately harmed members of his own community, the locals will normally provide food, drink, and lodging, hide him and his companions from the authorities, and offer whatever simple equipment or weapons they can spare. They may also provide willing helpers, including inexperienced young people eager to assist their local hero.

This support extends only as far as the community's resources permit and should not be treated as a source of unlimited equipment, wealth, or manpower.

**Restrictions:** The people of the Peasant Hero's home community regard him as one of their champions and frequently expect him to act on their behalf. When the community faces a serious threat or injustice, its people may appeal to him for assistance, whether the problem involves dangerous creatures, an abusive local authority, an unjust accusation, or some other crisis.

A Peasant Hero who repeatedly refuses reasonable requests for aid may lose the respect of his community. Until he has restored his standing, he suffers a –2 reaction adjustment when dealing with peasants who know of his reputation.

**Additional Notes:** The Peasant Hero's home community should be established when the character is created. It may be a village, farming settlement, fishing community, remote hamlet, or similar rural community. The character's relationship with his family and neighbours should be considered part of the kit's background.

The archetype is particularly appropriate to humans, halflings, and half-elves raised among human rural communities, but any race may produce Peasant Heroes where the campaign's culture supports the concept.

#### Outlaw or Pirate

**Description:** The Outlaw or Pirate is a warrior who rejects the authority of established rulers and laws and lives by his own code. A Pirate makes his living on the sea, raiding ships and coastal settlements; an Outlaw lives beyond the protection of the law, often in forests or other wilderness, and preys upon travellers and commerce.

Pirates and Outlaws may be ruthless criminals, rebels fighting an oppressive regime, or anything between. Some become celebrated heroes among those who suffer under the authorities they oppose. Others simply take what they want from those who possess it.

**Role:** The Pirate or Outlaw may be played as either a heroic rebel or a self-serving criminal. A heroic Pirate or Outlaw generally follows a strict personal code, protecting the innocent and opposing those he considers unjust. The classic outlaw may rob the wealthy to aid the poor, while a Pirate might refuse to harm passengers or crew who are not legitimate targets.

A character's alignment should reflect his actual conduct rather than the romantic image of the Pirate or Outlaw. A character who joins a company whose values differ greatly from his own may eventually find himself opposed by his own companions.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** **Pirate:** Required: Cutlass, Belaying Pin or Gaff/Hook; Recommended: Weapons suitable for boarding actions and shipboard combat. **Outlaw:** No specific requirements unless the campaign establishes a characteristic weapon associated with its outlaws, in which case the DM may require that weapon; Recommended: Long Bow, Long Sword, Quarterstaff.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** **Pirate:** Bonus: Rope Use, Seamanship; Recommended: Swimming, Weather Sense, Navigation, Engineering (for shipbuilding), Reading/Writing (for mapmaking), Appraising, Set Snares, Tightrope Walking, Tumbling. **Outlaw:** Bonus: Direction Sense, Fire-Building; Recommended: Riding (Land-Based), Animal Lore, Bowyer/Fletcher, Endurance, Hunting, Running, Set Snares, Survival, Tracking, Healing, Herbalism, Local History, Disguise.

**Equipment:** Heavy metal armour is generally unsuitable for either form of the kit. A Pirate wearing such armour risks being unable to swim or being dragged beneath the water if he falls overboard, while an Outlaw living permanently in the wilderness may find heavy armour difficult to maintain and transport. Such armour is not prohibited, however, and may be appropriate when the character expects a particular engagement or has access to suitable storage and maintenance.

**Benefits:** The Pirate or Outlaw has no intrinsic special benefits. The DM may establish campaign-specific advantages arising from the character's organization, reputation, or territory. A pirate campaign might include a hidden port or pirate settlement where stolen goods can be traded and where the authorities have little power. An outlaw campaign might provide safe houses, sympathetic communities, or allies among those opposed to the ruling authorities.

**Restrictions:** The authorities consider the Pirate or Outlaw a criminal and may actively seek his arrest or death. Bounties, warrants, informants, patrols, naval forces, bounty hunters, and other agents of the law may pursue him. The severity and persistence of this threat depend upon the character's crimes, reputation, and the resources available to the authorities.

A Pirate or Outlaw who is captured may face imprisonment, execution, forced labour, or other penalties appropriate to the laws of the campaign. The character should therefore take care when entering settlements where his identity or reputation is known.

**Additional Notes:** The distinction between a Pirate and an Outlaw is primarily one of environment and organization.

#### Samurai/Ronin

**Requirements:**

Ability: Strength 13+, Constitution 13+, Intelligence 14+, Wisdom 13+;

Alignment: Any Lawful;

Class: Fighter, Ranger, Paladin.

**Description:** The Samurai is a warrior bound by a strict code of honour demanding loyalty to his lord, readiness to sacrifice himself for his lord or his honour, willingness to avenge dishonour to his lord, family, or himself, faithful repayment of debts, and an absolute rejection of cowardice.

A Samurai may lose his position through the destruction of his lord's household, rejection by his lord, refusal to obey an order, or some other matter of honour. A Samurai who becomes masterless is known as a **Ronin**. A Ronin retains his training and abilities but is no longer bound to a lord. A Ronin may become a Samurai again by swearing allegiance to a suitable lord.

**Role:** The Samurai provides a warrior archetype based on a distinct martial culture and code of honour. In a campaign outside such a culture, the Samurai may provide an exotic perspective and a source of cultural conflict as well as a formidable warrior.

The player and DM should establish the character's cultural background, lord, obligations, and understanding of honour when the character is created.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** The Samurai/Ronin receives two additional weapon proficiency slots at character creation. Required: Katana (Specialization, 2 slots), Daikyu (Specialization, 3 slots).

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Etiquette, Riding (Land-Based); Required: Reading/Writing (Priest and Wizard, 2 slots unless Paladin or Ranger); Recommended: Artistic Ability/Calligraphy, Artistic Ability/Painting, Blind-Fighting, Running.

**Equipment:** The Samurai/Ronin must purchase starting equipment from the Samurai weapons, armour, and equipment available in the Equipment chapter and may have no more than 10 gp remaining after these purchases. The character receives a katana without charge.

**Benefits:** The Samurai/Ronin can focus his vital energy to temporarily increase his physical strength. Once per day per experience level, he may increase his Strength to 18/00 for one round. He must precede this exertion with a loud _kiai_ shout, making the ability impossible to use silently or without drawing attention.

For that round, all effects of Strength, including attack and damage adjustments, weight allowance, maximum press, open doors, and bend bars/lift gates, are calculated as though the character had Strength 18/00.

**Restrictions:** **Samurai:** A Samurai is bound by an oath of absolute loyalty to his lord and is expected to obey his lord's commands, even when those commands conflict with his personal interests or relationships. Refusing a legitimate command constitutes a serious dishonour. Depending upon the traditions of the character's culture, the expected consequence may be ritual suicide or the loss of status and becoming Ronin.

The DM should establish the nature of the Samurai's obligations when the character is created. These obligations may occasionally conflict with the character's personal goals, but should not be used simply to deprive the player of meaningful agency.

**Ronin:** A Ronin lacks the patronage and social position of a Samurai and advances at half the normal experience rate. This restriction ends if the Ronin swears allegiance to a suitable lord and becomes a Samurai again. The character then becomes subject to the obligations of a Samurai.

#### Savage

**Requirements:**

Ability: Strength 11+, Constitution 15+;

Class: Fighter, Ranger, Barbarian.

**Description:** The Savage is a warrior from a tribal culture that is notably more primitive than most. He is deeply familiar with the natural world and possesses the practical skills necessary to survive with limited technology and resources.

A Savage may come from almost any sort of tribal culture. His people may be noble and honourable, brutal and warlike, peaceful and communal, or anything in between. What distinguishes the Savage is not the morality of his people but the depth of his connection to their traditional way of life.

**Role:** The Savage brings practical wilderness skills and an unusual understanding of the natural world to an adventuring party. He may also serve as a voice of conscience, particularly when the supposedly civilized members of the party behave in ways his people would consider dishonourable or cruel.

The Savage is also an opportunity for role-playing centred on the meeting of very different cultures. The player should consider how his character regards the customs, technology, wealth, laws, and social conventions of the wider world.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** Required: The character's initial weapon proficiencies must be selected from a group of weapons designated by the DM as appropriate to his tribe. Typical choices include Blowgun, Long Bow, Short Bow, Club, Dagger, Javelin, Knife, Sling, and Spear. After play begins, the Savage may learn any other weapon for which he receives training.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Direction Sense, Weather Sense, Endurance, Survival; Recommended: Animal Handling, Animal Training, Fire-Building, Fishing, Riding (Land-Based), Rope Use, Swimming, Animal Lore, Bowyer/Fletcher, Hunting, Mountaineering, Running, Set Snares, Tracking, Healing, Herbalism, Local History, Religion, Jumping, Tightrope Walking, Tumbling.

**Equipment:** The Savage begins with no starting wealth. He receives four weapons from the list designated by the DM for weapon proficiencies.

He may also begin play with up to ten additional items accumulated during his life with his tribe, subject to DM approval. These must be items that members of his tribe could reasonably have made or acquired, such as clothing, pouches, food, rope, fishing equipment, or weapon sheaths. Manufactured goods requiring advanced technology are not normally permitted.

With the DM's approval, a Savage from a river or riding culture may instead begin with a small canoe or a riding horse with saddle blanket, halter, bit, and bridle.

**Benefits:** The Savage receives an unusually broad range of practical skills, represented by his four bonus nonweapon proficiencies.

In addition, the Savage possesses one innate ability selected from the following list when the character is created. The choice cannot later be changed. The ability may be used once per day per experience level. It is not magical and is not detected by _Detect Magic_. It requires no components, regardless of any components required by the corresponding spell.

- **Alarm:** The Savage may use this ability only while resting or sleeping in a quiet place. It does not produce an audible alarm. Instead, it alerts an awake Savage to activity within 10 feet or awakens him if he is asleep. It is not placed upon a particular location.
    
- **Detect Magic:** The Savage senses the presence of magic or other unnatural forces within the normal area and duration of the corresponding spell. Unless he is a ranger or a multiclass with access to magic, he cannot determine the school or type of magic involved.
    
- **Animal Friendship:** The Savage may use this ability on an animal that is not angry or threatened, causing it to become friendly. He may instead calm an angry or threatened animal, but must then use a second daily use to establish friendship. The Savage must confront the animal directly and be within the animal's attack range. As with the spell, the Savage cannot successfully use this ability if he harbours an ulterior motive toward the animal.
    
- **Detect Evil:** The Savage may detect evil in creatures, places, or magical items as described for the corresponding spell, but cannot use this ability to detect evil in player characters.
    

The DM may disallow one or more of these abilities or substitute another ability of similar power. Any substitute should be comparable to a 1st-level spell.

**Restrictions:** The Savage is uncomfortable with clothing that is significantly more cumbersome or concealing than his customary tribal dress. While wearing such clothing, he suffers a –1 penalty to attack rolls, damage rolls, and nonweapon proficiency checks.

The Savage may wear armour, but finds it profoundly uncomfortable. While wearing any armour, he suffers a –3 penalty to attack rolls, damage rolls, and nonweapon proficiency checks. This penalty represents more than unfamiliarity: the character's normal way of moving and fighting is fundamentally incompatible with wearing armour.

The Savage is expected to regard these restrictions as part of his character rather than simply accepting the penalties as the price of wearing equipment. The DM may impose appropriate additional consequences if the character consistently ignores the discomfort for extended periods.

**Additional Notes:** Demihumans are often thought of as more cultured and refined than humans owing to their longevity, but pockets of individuals might still live primitively.

#### Swashbuckler

**Requirements:**

Ability: Dexterity 13+, Intelligence 13+;

Class: Fighter, Ranger

**Description:** The Swashbuckler is a sophisticated, witty, lightly armed and armoured hero, most at home in the courts, streets, and salons of a great city. He relies upon agility, confidence, charm, and skill rather than heavy armour and brute force. Although capable of donning heavy armour and taking up a bastard sword when circumstances demand it, the Swashbuckler excels when lightly equipped and fighting with speed and finesse.

**Role:** The Swashbuckler is the quick-witted hero with the flashing blade and ready reply. He is equally at home duelling in a palace courtyard, pursuing a villain through crowded streets, or standing alongside more heavily armed warriors on the battlefield.

His intelligence and social confidence often make him a natural spokesman for the adventuring party and, in some circumstances, its leader. His charm can also make him particularly effective when dealing with NPCs who might otherwise be hostile or suspicious.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Two weapon proficiency slots, both of which must be devoted to one of Stiletto, Main-Gauche, Rapier, or Sabre; Recommended: These four weapons.

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:** Bonus: Etiquette, Tumbling; Recommended: Artistic Ability, Dancing, Heraldry, Languages (Modern), Riding (Land-Based), Seamanship, Blind-Fighting, Gaming, Musical Instrument, Reading/Writing, Appraising, Disguise, Forgery, Juggling, Tightrope Walking.

**Equipment:** The Swashbuckler must purchase at least one of the weapons in which he has specialized. Otherwise, he may spend his starting wealth normally.

**Benefits:** The Swashbuckler has access to the rogue nonweapon proficiency group and does not pay the additional proficiency-slot cost normally required for these proficiencies.

When wearing no armour, leather armour, or padded armour, the Swashbuckler receives a –2 bonus to Armour Class. His speed and agility make him exceptionally difficult to hit.

The Swashbuckler is also an inherently romantic figure. He receives a +2 reaction adjustment from NPCs who are romantically attracted to his gender, provided the circumstances allow such an attraction to influence the interaction.

**Restrictions:** The Swashbuckler must devote at least half of all weapon proficiency slots gained to Stiletto, Main-Gauche, Rapier, or Sabre until he has achieved proficiency in all four weapons. Once proficient in all four, he must progress towards Grand Mastery in one of them at the first opportunity.

The Swashbuckler has a tendency to become involved in dramatic situations. Rivals may challenge him to duels, strangers may seek his assistance in matters of honour, fugitives may seek his protection, and other complications may arise simply because he happens to be present when something interesting occurs. The DM should use such incidents to create opportunities for action and role-playing rather than as arbitrary punishments.

**Additional Notes:** The Swashbuckler is particularly appropriate to urban and courtly campaigns, but the archetype can exist wherever a culture values sophisticated warriors, personal duelling, wit, and social confidence.
