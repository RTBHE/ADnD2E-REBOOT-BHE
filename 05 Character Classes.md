# Chapter 5: Character Classes

After choosing your character’s race, you select his character class. A character class represents a character’s profession, training, and the body of knowledge and skills he has developed prior to beginning an adventuring career. Each character is assumed to have received instruction or experience sufficient to survive early adventuring life. Advancement thereafter is achieved through experience gained during play.

Character classes are divided into five class groups according to general vocation: warrior, priest, wizard, rogue, and adept. Each group shares similar patterns of advancement, including Hit Dice progression, combat ability, and saving throw advancement. Within each group are individual classes that define specific abilities, restrictions, and special features.

Each player selects a class group, then chooses a specific class within that group.

Fighter, mage, cleric, and thief are the standard character classes. They represent broadly applicable archetypes suitable for most campaign worlds. All other classes are optional and may be included or excluded at the Dungeon Master’s discretion depending on the campaign setting.

## Class Overview

Each class group is described briefly below. Full mechanical details are presented in later sections.

#### Warriors

Warriors are trained in combat, tactics, endurance, and the use of weapons and armour. They rely primarily on physical ability and martial skill rather than magic or subtlety.

The **fighter** is a general combatant skilled in weapons and battlefield tactics.

The **barbarian** is a primal warrior shaped by harsh environments and direct survival, relying on instinct and physical power.

The **paladin** is a holy warrior dedicated to ideals of righteousness, discipline, and divine purpose.

The **ranger** is a wilderness warrior skilled in survival, tracking, and skirmishing.

#### Priests

Priests serve as intermediaries between mortal society and higher spiritual or cosmological powers. Through devotion, ritual, and sacred discipline, they channel divine magic in accordance with their faith or spiritual tradition.

The **cleric** is a generalist priest of organized religion, serving temples, pantheons, or structured faiths.

The **druid** is a priest of natural balance and primal forces, devoted to the cycles of life, death, and renewal.

A **priest of a specific mythos** represents specialized religious traditions devoted to a single deity, principle, or supernatural power.

The **shaman** serves as an intermediary between the mortal world and the spirit realms, communicating with spirits, ancestors, and unseen forces.

#### Wizards

Wizards are arcane spellcasters who rely on study, knowledge, and disciplined research to shape magical forces into spells.

The **mage** is a generalist arcane caster trained in a wide range of magical disciplines.

**Specialist wizards** focus on a single school of magic, gaining greater mastery in exchange for reduced breadth.

#### Rogues

Rogues are defined by trained expertise, adaptability, and non-traditional solutions to problems. They rely on agility, perception, guile, charm, stealth, and specialized knowledge rather than direct martial or magical power.

The rogue group includes a wide range of callings: thieves, spies, scouts, infiltrators, investigators, burglars, smugglers, performers, diplomats, con artists, explorers, and many other roles that depend on skill rather than strength or formal authority.

Rogues are not defined by criminality. While some operate outside the law or at its margins, others work as legitimate professionals such as agents, guides, entertainers, or specialists in security and investigation. What unites rogues is method rather than morality or legality: they solve problems through skill, preparation, and opportunistic advantage.

All rogues share a common foundation of trained abilities expressed through a unified skill system, typically resolved using percentile dice. These abilities represent practised expertise rather than innate power, though magic, equipment, and circumstance may modify their effectiveness.

The thief, bard, and ninja represent different expressions of the rogue tradition:

- the **thief** as a specialist in stealth, infiltration, and acquisition
    
- the **bard** as a master of influence, knowledge, and reputation
    
- the **ninja** as a disciplined operative of concealment and precision    

#### Adepts

Adepts cultivate extraordinary abilities through rigorous physical, mental, and spiritual discipline. Rather than drawing power from martial training, arcane study, or divine favour, they perfect the latent potential within themselves through meditation, philosophy, ascetic practice, and unwavering self-mastery.

Although their disciplines differ, all adepts seek enlightenment through personal transformation. They value self-control, clarity of purpose, and harmony between body and mind, believing that true strength is attained not by external power but through mastery of the self.

- The **monk** pursues perfection through disciplined martial practice, developing exceptional speed, resilience, and unarmed combat techniques while following a philosophical path toward enlightenment.
    
- The **mystic** turns inward to awaken the hidden powers of the mind, cultivating extraordinary mental abilities through contemplation, willpower, and profound understanding of consciousness.

### Ability Score Requirements

Each character class has minimum ability score requirements. A character must meet these requirements to select that class.

If a character does not meet the requirements for any class, the Dungeon Master may allow ability scores to be adjusted or rerolled.

The Dungeon Master may also allow a character to increase one or more ability scores to meet the minimum requirements of a desired class. This is intended specifically to permit class qualification and should not normally result in ability scores exceeding the limits of character creation as determined by the campaign rules.

### Experience Points

Experience points represent a character’s growth in skill, knowledge, and capability through adventuring.

Characters earn experience by completing adventures and performing actions appropriate to their class. Different classes emphasize different forms of advancement: warriors gain more experience from combat, rogues from skill use and problem-solving, priests from service and spiritual deeds, and wizards from magical and intellectual achievements.

When sufficient experience is accumulated, a character advances in level and gains new abilities.

Some campaigns may require training time or other conditions for advancement, as determined by the Dungeon Master.

### Level

Level measures a character’s overall power and advancement. Unless the DM indicates an adventure will start at a higher level all characters begin at 1st level and improve through experience gained in play.

Each class progresses at a different rate and gains distinct abilities at higher levels.

### Prime Requisites

A classes’ prime requisites are the ability score or scores most important to that class. While specific class roles or play styles may rely on different skills, as a generalization:

- Warriors rely on Strength
    
- Priests rely on Wisdom
    
- Wizards rely on Intelligence
    
- Rogues rely on Dexterity or Charisma
    
- Adepts rely on Constitution

Prime requisites influence experience gain and class suitability.

A character who has a score of 16 or higher in all prime requisites for his class receives a 10% bonus to all experience point awards.

### Categories of Weapon Training

The training categories represent the degree of martial instruction required to learn a weapon effectively. These categories determine which weapons characters of a given class may select proficiency in. Individual classes, character kits, faiths, or other traditions may further restrict or expand these categories. A character trained in a broader category may select proficiency in any weapon belonging to a lesser category. The categories, from least to greatest, are:

**Simple weapons** - These require little formal instruction beyond that required for their ordinary use. They are commonly employed as tools, hunting implements, or the everyday arms of commoners, and may be used effectively with minimal training.

**Civilian weapons** - These are intended primarily for personal defence, hunting, protection from dangerous animals, law enforcement, or duelling. Their effective use requires dedicated instruction beyond that expected of ordinary labourers, but not the sustained training of organized military service.

**Militia weapons** - These require some organized martial instruction such as that provided to town watches, levies, hunting companies, or civic militias. They encompass weapons commonly employed in organized combat but not necessarily requiring the extensive battlefield training of professional soldiers.

**Military weapons** - These are the weapons of professional warfare and demand extensive martial training to be employed effectively. They include the most specialized battlefield arms, formation weapons, and heavy martial weapons.

The category of each specific weapon is given in the tables provided in the Weapons section of Chapter 8.

#### Exotic Weapons

Some weapons are designated as Exotic. Exotic weapons employ specialized techniques, uncommon designs, or culturally specific methods of use beyond those normally taught with their training category.

Proficiency in an exotic weapon should be discussed with the DM unless that proficiency is explicitly granted by a weapon group, class, kit, or other source. Some classes, kits, cultures, or traditions commonly provide training in exotic weapons. Even so, consultation with the DM is recommended to determine whether a particular exotic weapon is appropriate to the campaign and the character's background.

## Warriors

The warrior group encompasses those classes whose place in the world is determined primarily by martial skill, physical courage, discipline, and mastery of arms. Fighters, paladins, and rangers all belong to the warrior group, though each approaches the arts of war from a different tradition and philosophy.

Warriors are the most capable combatants in the game. They possess the broadest access to arms and armour, the greatest durability in battle, and the fastest progression in direct martial ability. While other classes may learn to fight competently, warriors are specialists in violence, battlefield survival, and armed conflict.

Warriors are expected to stand in danger where others cannot. Their class features and combat progression reflect not merely technical skill with weapons, but also battlefield awareness, endurance, aggression, and the ability to exploit fleeting opportunities in combat.

#### Weapons and Armour

Unlike members of other class groups, warriors are not ordinarily subject to broad restrictions on weapons or armour. Certain class abilities may require specific equipment or lighter encumbrance, and individual classes, kits, faiths, or other rules may impose additional limitations.

Fighters, paladins, and rangers may select proficiency in any non-exotic military weapon. Because military training encompasses all lesser categories, they may also learn any simple, civilian, or militia weapon.

Barbarians may select proficiency in any weapon. Exotic weapons remain subject to the approval of the DM and should ordinarily reflect the character's culture, upbringing, or experience.

#### Hit Dice and Constitution Bonuses

Warriors, except Barbarians, gain 1d10 hit points per level from 1st through 9th level. Barbarians use 1d12 instead. Constitution bonuses to hit points apply normally during these levels.

Beginning at 10th level, warriors no longer gain additional Hit Dice. Instead, they gain a flat 3 hit points per level thereafter, and no further Constitution bonuses are applied.

#### Magical Items and Spell Use

Warriors possess comparatively limited access to magical disciplines. Unless otherwise specified by class abilities, warriors cannot cast wizard spells and are restricted in their use of magical items intended for priests or wizards.

Paladins and rangers gain limited access to divine spellcasting at higher levels as described in their class entries.

#### Multiple Attacks

As warriors increase in experience, their growing combat skill allows them to strike more frequently in melee combat. This progression reflects increasing efficiency, aggression, and control in close combat rather than supernatural speed.

### Fighter

**Ability Requirement:** Strength 9

**Prime Requisite:** Strength

Fighters are professional men-at-arms, soldiers, mercenaries, knights, duelists, guardsmen, raiders, and warlords. More than any other class, the fighter is defined by practical mastery of violence. Where other warriors combine martial ability with religious devotion, wilderness skill, or supernatural gifts, the fighter relies upon training, discipline, experience, and battlefield judgment.

The fighter is the most flexible and broadly capable martial class. Fighters may be heavily armoured infantry, lightly equipped skirmishers, mounted lancers, archers, sailors, gladiators, disciplined pikemen, tribal champions, or hardened veterans of countless campaigns. The class imposes few assumptions about background, culture, or fighting style.

Strength is the principal attribute of the fighter, governing both accuracy and effectiveness in melee combat. A high Dexterity is also valuable, improving defence, missile combat, and battlefield survivability.

#### Weapon Specialization and Mastery

Fighter is the only class that is automatically eligible to pursue weapon specialization and weapon mastery.

Weapon specialization represents intensive training with a particular weapon or closely related group of weapons, granting improved accuracy, damage, speed, and combat efficiency. Higher degrees of mastery represent increasingly refined technical skill, battlefield familiarity, and instinctive command of a weapon.

A fighter is not required to specialize in any weapon. Many fighters instead choose broad proficiency across numerous arms, allowing them to adapt to changing circumstances and available equipment.

Rules for weapon proficiency, specialization, and mastery are presented in the Proficiencies chapter.

#### Magical Items

Although fighters cannot cast spells, they may make use of a wide variety of magical items. Fighters may use enchanted weapons, armour, shields, potions, protection scrolls, many rings, and other magical items not specifically restricted to priests or wizards.

Because fighters lack magical versatility, magical arms and armour often become an important part of their long-term advancement and battlefield effectiveness.

#### Strongholds and Followers

Upon reaching 9th level, a fighter’s reputation becomes sufficient to attract the service of professional soldiers, mercenaries, and lesser warriors seeking wealth, security, status, or the prestige of serving a renowned commander.

To attract such followers, the fighter must possess or establish a stronghold, fortress, manor, or similar domain capable of supporting armed retainers. Once established, warriors and men-at-arms begin entering the fighter’s service.

These followers are not automatons nor fanatics. They expect competent leadership, fair treatment, regular payment, and a reasonable expectation of survival and success. Poor leadership, abuse, unpaid wages, or catastrophic campaigns may result in desertion, indiscipline, or mutiny.

In addition to ordinary troops, the fighter also attracts a more loyal household guard or personal retinue composed of experienced and dependable soldiers. Such retainers expect superior treatment, equipment, and pay, but are generally more dependable than common mercenaries.

The composition of a fighter’s followers depends heavily upon the campaign setting, the fighter’s reputation, culture, alignment, and manner of rule. The DM should determine appropriate followers consistent with the surrounding world rather than relying solely upon random generation.

A fighter may own land, fortifications, or holdings before reaching 9th level. However, only upon attaining this degree of fame and reputation does the fighter naturally attract significant numbers of professional followers.

### Barbarian

**Ability Requirement****s****:** Strength 12, Dexterity 9, Constitution 12,

**Prime Requisite:** Strength, Constitution

**Races Allowed:** Human, Dwarf, Elf (Sylvan), Halfling (Furchin or Hardfell)

Barbarians are fierce warriors whose strength comes not from formal military training, but from lives spent surviving in harsh lands where endurance, instinct, and self-reliance matter more than discipline or wealth. Whether born among nomadic tribes, mountain clans, deep forests, or the frozen north, barbarians rely upon keen senses, exceptional toughness, and unwavering determination rather than sophisticated tactics or heavy armour.

Although often regarded by more settled peoples as uncultured or primitive, barbarians possess traditions, laws, and skills no less sophisticated than those of civilized lands. Their customs simply reflect different priorities, placing greater value upon courage, personal honour, and practical wisdom than upon learning, etiquette, or arcane scholarship. Many become adventurers after leaving their homelands as explorers, exiles, champions, mercenaries, or seekers of worthy challenges.

Barbarians distrust arcane magic, viewing it as an unpredictable force best approached with caution. While they readily accept the miracles of priests and the blessings of the gods, they remain deeply suspicious of enchanted devices whose workings they neither understand nor control.

**Hit Dice:** Barbarians use **d12** Hit Dice rather than the normal **d10** used by other warriors.

**Movement:** A barbarian's base movement rate is increased by **3**.

**Armour Limitation:** Barbarians may wear only padded, leather, studded leather, hide, or chain mail armour. They may use any shield.

**Combat Talents:** Barbarians automatically receive the **Trouble Sense** combat talent.

**Hardiness:** Barbarians receive a **+4 bonus on all Constitution** **checks**. This bonus applies whenever Constitution is used to resist fatigue, endure hardship, survive exposure, hold breath, swim long distances, or perform similar feats of endurance.

**Natural Athlete:** Barbarians begin play proficient in **Endurance**, **Running**, and **Swimming**, without expending proficiency slots.

**Indomitable Will:** Barbarians receive a **+2 bonus on saving throws** against fear, charm, illusion, domination, and other magical or mystic effects that seek to cloud the senses, overwhelm the will, or control the mind.

**Arcane Distrust:** Barbarians neither create nor willingly employ arcane magical items except under the most extraordinary circumstances. Potions, scrolls, and other single-use items may be used if absolutely necessary, but are generally regarded with suspicion. Divine magic and divine magical items carry no such stigma.

**Strongholds:** At 9th level a barbarian may establish a fortified hall, tribal settlement, or similar stronghold appropriate to his culture. Those drawn to serve him are typically warriors, hunters, families, and craftsmen who value strength, courage, and proven leadership over noble title or wealth.

### Paladin

**Ability Requirements:** Strength 12, Constitution 9, Wisdom 13, Charisma 17

**Prime Requisites:** Strength, Charisma

**Alignment:** Lawful Good

**Races Allowed:** Human (non-humans may be Demi-paladins as described at the end of the class description)

The paladin is a warrior bound to the service of law and righteousness through sacred oaths, religious devotion, and personal discipline. Paladins are expected to embody courage, justice, mercy, honesty, and self-sacrifice, standing as examples to both common folk and rulers alike. They are not merely skilled warriors, but champions entrusted with divine purpose and moral responsibility.

The path of the paladin is demanding. Many possess courage or martial skill, but far fewer can consistently uphold the discipline, restraint, humility, and moral certainty required of a true paladin. A paladin must remain lawful good in both conviction and conduct. This obligation extends beyond outward obedience to law and includes sincerity of purpose, respect for just authority, protection of the innocent, and opposition to cruelty, corruption, and evil.

Paladins are not expected to be naive, humourless, or incapable of practical judgment. They may employ caution, strategy, secrecy, or deception when necessary to oppose evil or protect the innocent. However, cruelty, treachery for personal gain, abuse of authority, knowingly consorting with evil, or the willing commission of evil acts are incompatible with the calling of the paladin.

#### Conduct and Fall from Grace

A paladin who knowingly commits a chaotic act must seek out a lawful good priest of at least 7th level, confess the transgression, and undertake whatever penance is required. Minor failings, errors in judgment, unavoidable lesser evils, or actions taken in ignorance do not automatically strip a paladin of status, though repeated misconduct, selfishness, or disregard for lawful conduct may place the character in spiritual peril.

A paladin who knowingly and willingly commits an evil act immediately loses paladin status. This fall is ordinarily irrevocable. The character forever after functions as a fighter of the same level, retaining existing experience points but permanently losing all paladin abilities and privileges. A fallen paladin may not thereafter gain weapon specialization or mastery beyond what had already been attained prior to the fall.

A paladin who commits evil while magically compelled, dominated, or otherwise deprived of meaningful free will loses access to paladin abilities until atonement is completed, but is not considered permanently fallen. Such a character functions as a fighter of equal level until the required penance, quest, or sacred duty is fulfilled. No experience points are gained while the paladin remains under such disgrace.

The DM should distinguish carefully between intentional evil, moral failure under pressure, tragic necessity, ignorance, manipulation, and honest disagreement over lawful conduct. The paladin is held to a high standard, but should not become unplayable through excessive moral absolutism or adversarial interpretation.

#### Detect Evil Intent

By concentrating for one full minute, a paladin may sense the presence of malign supernatural evil or deliberate hostile intent within 60 feet in a chosen direction.

This ability detects creatures, enchantments, or supernatural influences strongly aligned with evil, as well as individuals presently intending serious harm, betrayal, murder, or similarly malevolent acts. It does not reveal general selfishness, petty vice, political disagreement, or moral imperfection, nor does it automatically distinguish between lawful and chaotic evil.

The paladin may use this ability as often as desired.

#### Divine Protection

Paladins receive a +2 bonus to all saving throws.

In addition, a paladin is immune to all natural diseases. Supernatural afflictions such as lycanthropy, mummy rot, or curses are not prevented unless specifically noted otherwise.

#### Lay on Hands

A paladin may heal wounds through the laying on of hands. Once per day, the paladin may restore a total number of hit points equal to 2 points per level. This healing may be applied to the paladin or to another creature touched by the paladin.

The healing need not be granted all at once, but the full daily amount must be used within a single encounter or scene at the DM’s discretion.

#### Cure Disease

A paladin may cure disease through touch. This ability functions once per week for every five levels attained:

| Paladin Level | Uses per Week |
| ------------- | ------------- |
| 1–5           | 1             |
| 6–10          | 2             |
| 11–15         | 3             |
| 16–20         | 4             |

This ability cures diseases, poisons of biological origin, and similar bodily afflictions, but does not remove curses, magical transformations, or supernatural conditions unless specifically allowed by the DM.

#### Aura of Protection

Paladins are surrounded by a palpable aura of divine authority extending 10 feet in all directions. Summoned evil creatures and creatures of strongly evil supernatural nature suffer a –1 penalty to attack rolls while within this aura, regardless of their target.

Creatures affected by the aura instinctively recognize its source, even if the paladin is disguised or concealed.

#### Turning Undead and Fiends

Beginning at 3rd level, a paladin gains the ability to turn undead and fiends through force of faith and divine authority. The paladin turns such creatures as a priest two levels lower than the paladin’s current level.

More information on turning is found in the Priest Class Description and in the Combat chapter.

#### Bonded Mount

At 4th level or later, a paladin may undertake the calling or quest that leads to a sacred mount entering the paladin’s service. Although commonly a warhorse, the mount may instead be another creature appropriate to the paladin’s culture, faith, or divine patron.

The paladin does not simply summon the mount into existence. Rather, the bond is formed through providence, quest, vision, divine guidance, or extraordinary circumstance. Such mounts are exceptionally intelligent, loyal, and courageous companions rather than ordinary trained animals.

The exact nature of the mount is determined by the DM.

#### Divine Spellcasting

Beginning at 9th level, paladins gain the ability to cast priest spells. Unless their specific faith details otherwise, all Paladins gain major access to the spheres of Combat, Divination, Healing, and Protection.

Paladin spellcasting follows the normal rules for priestly magic except as follows:

- Paladins use the Paladin Spell Progression table.
    
- Paladins do not gain bonus spells for high Wisdom.
    
- Paladins cannot cast from priest scrolls unless otherwise permitted.
    
- Paladins may not use priest-exclusive magical items unless specifically allowed to warriors.

Paladin spells represent divine favour granted in recognition of continued devotion and faithful service rather than formal priestly training.

#### Holy Swords

If a paladin acquires and properly bears a holy sword, the weapon projects a circle of power with a 10-foot radius whenever drawn and wielded. Within this area, hostile magic may be dispelled as if by dispel magic cast at a level equal to the paladin’s own level.

Holy swords are exceptionally rare relics of divine significance, not ordinary magical weapons.

#### Restrictions and Obligations

The powers granted to a paladin are balanced by strict obligations of conduct, charity, discipline, and service.

#### Wealth and Possessions

A paladin may not hoard wealth or pursue riches for personal luxury. The paladin may retain sufficient funds to maintain equipment, support dependants and retainers, uphold obligations, construct or maintain a stronghold, and continue the practical work of the paladin’s calling.

Excess wealth must be donated to religious, charitable, or otherwise worthy causes consistent with lawful good principles. Such donations may not be used to circumvent these restrictions by transferring personal wealth to companions, retainers, or secondary holdings under the paladin’s indirect control.

#### Tithes

A paladin must tithe no less than one tenth of all income and treasure acquired. This tithe must be paid to a lawful good religious institution, sacred order, charitable cause, or similar organization at the earliest reasonable opportunity.

#### Magical Items

A paladin may possess no more than ten magical items in total. Of these, no more than:

- one suit of magical armour,
    
- one magical shield,
    
- four magical weapons,
    
- and four additional magical items

may be retained at any time. Magical ammunition does not count toward this limit.

Paladins are expected to treat magical items as sacred tools in service of duty rather than objects of greed or prestige.

#### Followers and Associates

Unlike fighters, paladins do not automatically attract mercenary followers upon establishing a stronghold. However, they may hire soldiers, servants, and specialists of suitable conduct and reputation.

A paladin will ordinarily accept companions of differing alignments provided they are not evil and do not habitually engage in cruelty, corruption, treachery, or wanton wrongdoing. The paladin is expected to oppose evil acts and discourage immoral conduct, but is not required to abandon every ally over minor failings, cultural differences, or moral imperfection.

Paladins rarely tolerate persistent association with knowingly evil individuals except under extraordinary circumstances directly tied to a greater good, redemption, or sacred duty.

**Why Only Humans?**

Many ask why only humans may become true paladins. The leading theory is that the gods recognize not merely virtue, but the triumph of virtue over adversity.

Among the elder races, wisdom comes with centuries of life, tradition is deeply rooted, and many possess gifts of body or spirit unknown to humanity. Humans possess none of these advantages. Their lives are brief, their passions fierce, and they must often choose between duty and ambition with little certainty or time.

To attain the uncompromising ideals demanded of a paladin under such conditions is, many theologians argue, a rarer and more remarkable achievement. It is not that elves, dwarves, or other peoples are less capable of goodness, but that they walk different paths toward it. The gods reserve the mantle of the paladin for those whose devotion shines brightest against the greatest uncertainty.

This does not diminish the champions of other races. Many become holy warriors of legendary renown, blessed by their deities and gifted with powers akin to those of a paladin. Such individuals are respected as peers by the great orders, though tradition distinguishes them as _demi-paladins_ rather than true paladins.

#### Demi-Paladins

Only humans may become true paladins. Exceptional demihumans may nevertheless devote themselves to the same ideals, earning divine blessings through years of faithful service. Such champions are known as demi-paladins.

Few demihumans possess the devotion required to walk this path. Those who do spend years proving themselves through faithful service before the gods grant even a single blessing. While a human paladin receives the full mantle of the calling upon entering the order, a demi-paladin earns each divine gift separately through a lifetime of trial.

A demihuman that intends to follow the path of paladinhood must meet the normal alignment and ability score prerequisites of a paladin at character creation, must multiclass as a fighter/cleric following all the standard rules of multiclassing, and must choose a paladin character kit approved by the DM.

From the start of play the demi-paladin must further adhere to the standards of conduct and observe all the restrictions and obligations of both the paladin class and their faith.

At cleric levels 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, and 14, the character must undertake a quest as detailed by the DM. Once the quest is successfully completed, the character is awarded one new paladin ability by the DM (i.e: a) the ability to use Paladin-items (including holy swords); b) detect evil intent; c) divine protection; d) lay on hands; e) cure disease; f) aura of protection; or g) bonded mount).

### Ranger

**Ability Requirements:** Strength 13, Dexterity 13, Constitution 14, Wisdom 14

**Prime Requisites:** Strength, Dexterity, Wisdom

**Alignment:** Any Good

**Races Allowed:** Human, Elf, Half-Elf

The ranger is a warrior of the wild frontiers: hunter, scout, tracker, warden, and wandering protector. Rangers survive through vigilance, fieldcraft, patience, and practical knowledge of the wilderness. Though formidable warriors, rangers rely as much upon stealth, endurance, and judgment as upon strength of arms.

Rangers are commonly associated with forests and unsettled lands, but the class is not limited to a single environment. A ranger may be a mountain scout, marsh guide, desert rider, frontier outrider, coastal warden, or hunter of dangerous creatures wherever civilization meets the untamed world.

All rangers are committed in some fashion to the protection of life, the defence of communities, or the opposition of predatory evil. Unlike paladins, however, rangers are not bound to rigid codes of law or religious hierarchy. Rangers often operate independently, beyond the reach of courts and kingdoms, and may employ stealth, ambush, deception, or irregular tactics where necessary.

#### **Arms and Armour**

Several ranger abilities function only while wearing studded leather armour or lighter and, if encumbrance rules are used, while carrying no more than a light encumbrance.

The ranger's skills depend upon mobility, awareness, and freedom of movement. Heavy armour hinders stealth, woodcraft, and the swift, fluid fighting techniques upon which many of the class's abilities rely.

#### Two-Weapon Fighting

When wearing studded leather armour or lighter, a ranger may fight with a weapon in each hand without suffering the normal penalties for two-weapon fighting. The ranger may not use a shield while fighting in this manner.

When wearing heavier armour, the ranger may still fight with two weapons, but suffers the standard penalties.

This ability reflects extensive training in aggressive skirmishing and mobile combat rather than supernatural speed or coordination.

#### Primary Terrain

While a Ranger’s skills are not limited to a specific terrain or environment, they naturally have greater knowledge and experience with the one where they have spent the most time. Character kits – if used – may grant additional benefits to Rangers operating in such terrain. The player should choose from one of the following broad options unless the DM provides a list better suited to the environment of their specific campaign world: Aquatic, Arctic, Desert, Forest, Hill, Jungle, Mountain, Plains, Swamp.

#### Favoured Enemy

Before advancing to 2nd level, every ranger must select a species or broad creature type that serves as the ranger’s principal enemy. Common choices include orcs, goblinoids, giants, trolls, undead, lizard men, or similar threats common to the campaign region and most often to the Ranger’s primary terrain. The DM must approve the choice.

When attacking creatures of the chosen type, the ranger gains a +4 bonus to attack rolls.

This hatred is rarely subtle. The ranger suffers a –4 penalty to reaction rolls when dealing with creatures of the hated type, and will ordinarily seek them out in battle ahead of other threats unless doing so would be tactically foolish or endanger innocents.

The favoured enemy represents long experience, bitter enmity, cultural duty, personal trauma, or dedicated study rather than supernatural destiny.

The DM may permit especially broad or especially narrow enemy choices only where appropriate to the campaign.

#### Animal Empathy

Rangers possess an instinctive understanding of natural animals and their behaviour.

Domestic animals and non-hostile wild creatures may generally be calmed, approached, handled, or befriended by a ranger without the need for a check under ordinary circumstances.

When dealing with frightened, aggressive, trained, or dangerous animals, the ranger may attempt to alter the creature’s reaction through careful behaviour and animal handling. The creature must make a saving throw versus rods. The saving throw suffers a –1 penalty, with an additional –1 penalty for every three ranger levels beyond 1st.

If the save fails, the ranger may shift the creature’s reaction one category in a favourable direction.

This ability is non-magical and does not grant direct communication, mind control, or supernatural command over animals.

The ranger must ordinarily approach openly and without obvious fear for the ability to function.

#### Divine Spellcasting

Beginning at 8th level, rangers gain limited access to priest spells. Rangers gain major access to the Animal and Plant spheres.

Ranger spellcasting follows the normal rules for priestly magic except as follows:

- Rangers use the Ranger Abilities Table.
    
- Rangers do not gain bonus spells for high Wisdom.
    
- Rangers may not use priest scrolls unless specifically permitted.
    
- Rangers may not use priest-exclusive magical items unless otherwise noted.

Ranger spells represent spiritual harmony with the natural world, instinctive wisdom, and the favour of powers tied to nature rather than formal priestly authority.

#### Followers

At 10th level, a ranger’s reputation and deeds attract loyal followers from among frontier folk, scouts, hunters, woodsmen, and creatures of the wild.

The ranger attracts 2d6 followers over the course of several months. These followers may include humans, demi-humans, animals, or more unusual beings associated with the wilderness and borderlands. Some may seek service directly, while others are encountered naturally during adventures.

The exact composition of these followers should reflect the campaign setting and the ranger’s personal history rather than relying solely upon random tables.

Followers are initially loyal, but their continued service depends upon the ranger’s conduct and leadership. Rangers gain no supernatural ability to communicate with animal followers beyond ordinary means.

A ranger is never required to accept followers and may release them freely.

Unlike fighters, rangers do not automatically attract soldiers through strongholds or political authority.

#### Conduct and Restrictions

Rangers must remain of good alignment. They are expected to oppose cruelty, predation, corruption, and needless destruction.

A ranger who knowingly and willingly commits an evil act permanently loses ranger status and thereafter functions as a fighter of equal level. Excess experience points beyond the maximum for that fighter level are lost.

A ranger who commits evil unwillingly, under coercion, magical influence, deception, or extreme necessity loses access to ranger abilities until the wrong is corrected and appropriate atonement completed. During this time the ranger gains no additional experience.

The exact nature of such atonement depends upon the offence committed and should arise naturally from the campaign rather than through arbitrary punishment.

#### Solitary Nature

Rangers are traditionally independent wanderers uncomfortable with courts, large organizations, and permanent authority. Until reaching 8th level, a ranger may not maintain permanent henchmen, mercenary companies, or large numbers of retainers.

This restriction does not prevent ordinary travelling companions, temporary allies, guides, or similar associates.

#### Wealth

Rangers do not ordinarily accumulate large stores of wealth or luxury possessions. A ranger may possess whatever equipment and treasure can reasonably be carried, transported, or actively maintained.

Excess wealth is generally converted into practical portable valuables, expended in pursuit of the ranger’s duties, distributed among companions, or donated to worthy causes and communities.

Rangers are not forbidden from owning property or maintaining hidden refuges, cabins, lodges, or similar holdings, but they rarely become sedentary nobles or wealthy landholders.

## Priests

Priests are intermediaries between the mortal world and the powers beyond it. Through devotion, ritual, discipline, sacrifice, and sacred knowledge, priests gain the ability to invoke divine magic and act in service of religious, spiritual, or cosmological principles greater than themselves.

The priest group encompasses many different traditions. Clerics and priests of a specific mythos serve organized religions, temples, and established pantheons; druids preserve ancient natural and cosmic orders; shamans mediate between mortals and the spirit world through ecstatic rites, visions, and ancestral traditions. Though their practices differ greatly, all priests derive power from faith, spiritual authority, and recognized connection to the higher powers and structures underlying the cosmos.

Priests are not merely believers. Common worshippers may pray, sacrifice, or observe religious customs without possessing any divine power whatsoever. A priest is instead one who has been entrusted with sacred authority and granted the ability to act directly as an agent of divine or spiritual forces.

Priests combine spiritual power with practical capability. Though less specialized in combat than warriors, priests are expected to defend their faith, protect their followers, confront supernatural threats, and survive the dangers associated with their calling.

#### Divine Magic

All priests cast divine spells. Unlike wizardly magic, priestly magic is not produced through personal manipulation of arcane forces, but through alignment with the greater powers and principles that shape the structure of the multiverse itself.

The powers worshipped by priests—whether gods, pantheons, or sacred natural forces—are understood by many scholars and theologians to be connected fundamentally to the “warp” of the Weave: the ordering structure that grants meaning, identity, purpose, and form to existence.

Through prayer, ritual, devotion, sacrifice, and obedience to sacred obligations, priests learn to invoke and direct these forces in limited ways. Divine magic is therefore neither purely external nor purely personal. A priest does not simply command power, but acts as an intermediary through whom higher structures of reality are expressed.

Because divine magic depends upon spiritual alignment and sacred relationship rather than technical formulae, priests are constrained by doctrine, obligation, taboo, and the expectations of the powers they serve. These differing relationships to divine power are represented mechanically through categories known as spheres of influence. A priest’s class or specific faith determines which spheres are available and whether access is major or minor.

The spheres are:

- All
    
- Animal
    
- Astral
    
- Charm
    
- Combat
    
- Creation
    
- Divination
    
- Elemental
    
- Guardian
    
- Healing
    
- Necromantic
    
- Plant
    
- Protection
    
- Summoning
    
- Sun
    
- Weather

Additional spheres may exist in particular campaign settings or mythos.

While all priests invoke divine magic through faith and sacred authority, some traditions—most notably shamans—also cultivate relationships with lesser spiritual beings that may grant additional supernatural aid.

Divine magic should not be confused with mysticism. Priests invoke powers associated with the ordering structure of the cosmos itself, while mystics cultivate mastery over the essential cohesion that exists between the threads of the Weave. Though both may produce supernatural effects, their methods, philosophies, and underlying principles are fundamentally different.

#### Clerics, Druids, and Other Priests

Not all priests serve religion in the same manner.

**Clerics** are the most common and broadly trained form of priest. Rather than serving a single deity in isolation, they serve an entire religious tradition or pantheon. Though a cleric may personally favour particular gods or forces, he is expected to understand and uphold the broader sacred order recognized by that tradition.

In practical terms, clerics are generalists within their religious tradition, much as mages are generalists among arcane practitioners.

**Priests of a Specific Mythos**, by contrast, are devoted exclusively to a single deity or cosmic power. The ethos of their chosen faith governs every aspect of their ministry.

**Druids** are one example of such priests. They serve the forces of nature—ancient mysteries, seasonal cycles, sacred landscapes, and the primal balances that bind the ordered structure of the cosmos to the living world.

**Shamans** are likewise devoted exclusively to a specific faith, but they do not operate within the structures of organized religion. Instead, they commune with spirits, who serve as intermediaries between the shaman and the divine.

The exact structure and role of priesthoods varies greatly between cultures and mythologies.

#### Arms and Armour

As protectors of their faith and flock, most priests receive martial training, though not to the same degree as warriors. Subject to any requirements or restrictions imposed by their faith and character kit, they may select proficiency in non-exotic militia weapons. Likewise, they may wear any armour and use shields where such equipment does not offend the tenets of their faith.

If the restrictions imposed by a faith and a character kit conflict, those of the faith always take precedence. Likewise, any restriction imposed by a character kit takes precedence over any additional permissions granted by the faith.

Some priesthoods forbid particular classes of weapons, others require the use of specific sacred arms, while still others regard extensive martial training as an expression of devotion. Such restrictions and requirements are described in the individual class and faith entries.

**Shamans** follow these rules except that they may also select proficiency in exotic weapons and are always permitted certain additional weapons regardless of faith or character kit. Otherwise, they remain subject to the restrictions imposed by both. Their armour is further limited as described in the class description.

#### Hit Dice and Constitution Bonuses

Priests gain 1d8 hit points per level from 1st through 9th level unless their faith specifies a different size die. Shaman use a die one size larger than a priest of the same faith (typically d10). Constitution bonuses apply normally during these levels.

Beginning at 10th level, priests no longer gain additional Hit Dice. Instead, they gain a flat 2 hit points per level thereafter, and no further Constitution bonuses are applied.

#### Wisdom and Bonus Divine Spell Points

Clerics, druids, and priests of a specific mythos gain additional Divine Spell Points from high Wisdom. Shamans do not.

This bonus reflects increased spiritual attunement, allowing a more stable connection to divine authority rather than improved memory or intellect.

### Cleric

**Ability Requirements:** Wisdom 9

**Prime Requisites:** Wisdom

**Races Allowed:** Any

Clerics are trained intermediaries of established religious traditions who serve as defenders of their communities. Their role is not merely devotional, but protective: they preserve the spiritual, social, and metaphysical stability of the world according to their society's understanding of the divine order.

Within most cultures, clerics serve entire pantheons, syncretic religious systems, or broader sacred traditions rather than a single deity or faith. These traditions encompass multiple divine beings, aspects, or principles within a unified cosmological order. While individual clerics may hold personal devotion to particular gods or spirits, their authority derives from their place within a wider religious structure rather than exclusive service to a single patron.

Where priests devoted exclusively to a single deity act primarily as guardians of doctrinal purity and the integrity of their specific faith, clerics are generalists tasked with sustaining the broader spiritual and communal framework within which diverse beliefs coexist.

Clerics combine spiritual authority with practical capability. They are expected to defend the faithful, protect settlements, oppose supernatural threats, and maintain the stability of the communities that depend upon their guidance.

Clerics therefore concern themselves not only with the will of the gods, but with maintaining the harmony between divine powers upon which the stability of mortal society depends.

#### Weapons

A cleric’s weapon use is shaped by religious practice rather than martial doctrine alone. Reflecting both the symbolic and practical demands of their role within a shared sacred order, clerics are restricted to bludgeoning weapons.

Bludgeoning weapons are understood as the most direct and least symbolically disruptive form of violence available to mortals. They avoid the symbolic separation inherent in shedding blood between peoples who may all fall under overlapping divine jurisdictions within a broader pantheon or sacred system.

This restriction also reflects the cleric's role as a mediator within a multi-deity or multi-aspect religious framework, where restraint in the use of violence helps preserve harmony between competing divine influences.

#### Divine Magic

Clerics cast divine spells through alignment with the ordering structures of the cosmos as expressed through their faith tradition.

In cosmological terms, clerical magic arises from the “warp” of the Weave—the structural principle that gives form, meaning, and purpose to reality. Clerics act as conduits through which these higher ordering forces are expressed in the mortal world.

Clerics are generalists among priests, with broad access to divine spheres of influence. Their magic reflects the needs of organized religious life: healing, protection, guidance, judgment, purification, and the containment or correction of supernatural disorder.

Clerics have major access to all spheres except Animal, Plant, Weather, and Elemental, and minor access to the Elemental sphere.

Divine magic is not a transactional relationship with a single patron, but a structured alignment between the cleric, their faith tradition, and the broader cosmological order that faith interprets and serves.

#### Turning Undead

Clerics are granted authority over undead creatures—entities that represent disruption or inversion of the proper relationship between form, essence, and spiritual continuity.

Undead are treated as violations of the intended balance of existence, whether arising from necromantic corruption, cursed persistence, or metaphysical rejection of death’s proper role.

Clerics may repel, command, or destroy undead depending on alignment, doctrine, and circumstance. Evil-aligned clerics may instead assert dominion over such forces, reflecting alternate theological interpretations of authority over death and unlife.

#### Followers and Temple Authority

At higher levels, clerics who establish a recognized place of worship may attract followers drawn from the faithful, temple staff, militia, and lay adherents of their tradition.

These followers represent the cleric’s role as a stabilizing figure within a living religious community. Their loyalty is grounded in faith, social order, and institutional authority rather than personal obedience alone.

The number and nature of followers depend on the cleric’s reputation, deeds, and religious context, and they arrive gradually over time rather than as a single organised force.

Additional retainers may be hired, but do not possess the same inherent loyalty as devoted followers.

#### Religious Strongholds

High-level clerics may establish formal religious strongholds such as temples, abbeys, monasteries, shrines, or sanctuaries.

At 9th level, such institutions may receive formal recognition from the cleric’s religious tradition, granting institutional support such as reduced construction costs, donated labour, or protective influence depending on the nature of the faith.

Earlier construction is possible, but without recognition these holdings function as private expressions of faith rather than sanctioned institutions of the broader religious order.

### Druid

**Ability Requirements:** **Wisdom 12, Charisma 15**

**Prime Requisite:** **Wisdom, Charisma**

**Alignment:** True Neutral

**Races Allowed:** Human, Half-Elf

Druids are priests of the natural order and guardians of the cycles that sustain the living world. Where clerics preserve the stability of mortal communities through organized religion, druids concern themselves with the balance of the greater natural and cosmic systems upon which all life depends.

To the druid, civilization is neither inherently good nor inherently corrupt. Settlements, agriculture, hunting, war, growth, decay, and death are all parts of the natural cycle. Druids become hostile only when these cycles are disrupted through reckless exploitation, unnatural corruption, or attempts to impose permanent dominion over forces that must remain in balance.

Druids are also an example of a priesthood defined by a specific mythos. Their powers arise not from service to civic pantheons or institutional religion, but from attunement to the primal order of the living world itself: the cycles of season, weather, growth, death, and renewal.

#### Arms and Armour

Druids reject worked metal armour and most heavily manufactured weapons, viewing them as symbols of excessive separation from the natural world and the cycles that sustain mortal life.

A druid may wear only padded, leather, or hide armour, and may use only wooden shields. Magical versions of otherwise permitted armour and shields are allowed provided they remain fundamentally natural in form.

A druid may take proficiency only in weapons that are primarily fashioned from natural materials or commonly associated with hunting, husbandry, ritual practice, or simple rural craftsmanship. Weapons whose construction depends upon extensive metalworking, complex mechanisms, or the traditions of organized warfare are forbidden. Examples of the weapons normally available to druids are the club, dagger, dart, scimitar, sickle, sling, spear, and quarterstaff.

#### Divine Magic

Druidic magic reflects attunement to the living and elemental forces of the natural world rather than the ordered structures emphasized by clerics.

Druids have major access to the spheres of:

- All
    
- Animal
    
- Elemental
    
- Healing
    
- Plant
    
- Weather
  
They have minor access to the Divination sphere.

Druids do not receive the broader protective and institutional spell access typical of clerics, but instead command powerful magics relating to nature, growth, weather, beasts, and elemental forces.

Unlike clerics, druids cannot turn undead.

#### Druidic Language

All druids know a secret druidic tongue in addition to their normal languages. This language does not require a proficiency slot.

The language is intentionally limited in vocabulary and structure, focusing primarily upon nature, weather, geography, living things, ritual practice, and druidic philosophy. Druids guard it carefully as a means of recognition and communication within their order.

#### Woodland Affinity

Beginning at **3rd level**, druids may identify natural plants, animals, and pure water with perfect accuracy.

At the same level, druids may move through natural overgrowth—including briars, dense brush, thorn thickets, and similar terrain—at normal movement speed and without leaving a trail.

Also beginning at 3rd level, druids gradually learn the languages of woodland and wilderness beings. A druid learns one such language at 3rd level and one additional language each level thereafter. Typical choices include the tongues of dryads, treants, centaurs, fauns, pixies, sprites, and similar creatures tied closely to the natural world.

#### Resistance to Enchantment

At **7th level**, druids become immune to charm effects cast by woodland creatures such as dryads, nixies, and similar beings.

#### Shapechanging

Beginning at **7th level**, a druid gains the ability to assume the form of natural animals.

The druid may shapechange up to three times per day:

- once into a mammal,
    
- once into a bird,
    
- and once into a reptile.

The chosen form may range in size from very small creatures to approximately the size of a black bear. The druid gains the movement abilities, natural attacks, armour Class, and physical capabilities of the assumed form, but retains his own mind, personality, and hit points.

Upon assuming a new form, the druid also heals 1d6 × 10% of all current damage suffered.

Clothing and one handheld item in each hand merge into the new form and reappear when the druid returns to normal shape.

#### Ethos

Druids must remain true neutral in alignment.

This neutrality does not imply indifference or passivity. Druids may oppose tyranny, defend the innocent, wage war against corruption, or ally temporarily with powerful forces of good or evil. However, druids reject ideologies that seek absolute dominance, permanent stasis, reckless destruction, or unnatural disruption of the world’s essential cycles.

Druids are expected to protect wilderness, preserve natural balance, and resist exploitation that threatens long-term ecological or metaphysical stability.

They are often patient, subtle, and unforgiving in pursuing these goals.

#### Mistletoe and Sacred Symbols

Mistletoe serves as a primary sacred symbol of the druidic tradition and is required for many druidic spells.

Traditionally, ritual mistletoe is harvested beneath the full moon using a specially prepared golden or silver sickle. Improperly gathered mistletoe weakens the effectiveness of spells requiring it, typically granting targets improved saving throws or reducing spell potency at the DM’s discretion.

#### Druidic Hierarchy

Unlike clerics, druids belong to a highly structured initiatory hierarchy tied to geographic regions and sacred natural authority.

At lower levels, druids are considered initiates within the broader order. Advancement beyond 11th level requires acceptance into the ruling circles of the druidic hierarchy, which maintain strict limits on the number of druids permitted at each rank within a given region.

Positions within the hierarchy are traditionally earned through challenge, succession, recognition, or vacancy rather than simple accumulation of experience alone. Such contests are not always lethal, but they are always binding.

As druids rise in rank they attract initiates, attendants, and lesser druids who serve under their authority within the natural order.

#### The Grand Druid and Hierophants

At the highest levels of the hierarchy stand the Great Druids, the Grand Druid, and finally the Hierophant Druids who transcend ordinary mortal concerns and become living embodiments of druidic philosophy.

Hierophants no longer gain additional spell progression, instead acquiring increasingly profound supernatural abilities tied to transcendence of mortality, elemental attunement, and union with the deeper natural order.

These powers include:

- immunity to natural poisons,
    
- freedom from aging infirmity,
    
- alteration of appearance,
    
- hibernation,
    
- and eventual ability to enter and survive within the Elemental Planes.

The precise structure and advancement of the higher druidic hierarchy is detailed in the accompanying tables and hierarchy rules.

### Priest of a Specific Mythos

**Ability Requirements:** Varies (Minimum Wisdom 9+)

**Prime Requisites:** per selected faith

**Alignment:** per selected faith

**Races Allowed:** per selected faith

In some campaigns, there are priests who do not serve broad pantheons or general religious traditions, but instead belong to tightly defined faiths dedicated to a single deity, spirit, or supernatural power. These are collectively referred to as priests of a specific mythos.

Unlike clerics, who operate as generalist priests within larger religious systems, priests of a specific mythos derive their abilities entirely from the doctrines, strictures, and metaphysical relationship of a single faith. Their powers, limitations, and role in the world are defined by that faith rather than by a broader religious framework.

Such priests are not separate classes, but a specialized expression of the priest group. Their differences from clerics arise from their faith rather than from their fundamental nature as priests.

The structure, obligations, and capabilities of a priest of a specific mythos are determined by the details of the individual faith, which should be defined in cooperation between player and Dungeon Master.

At minimum, each mythos should define:

- **Requirements** (including ability scores, alignment constraints, or other conditions)
    
- **Arms and Armour Restrictions**
    
- **Spell Access** (via spheres of influence and any modifications to progression)
    
- **Granted Powers** (unique abilities or divine effects)
    
- **Ethos** (behavioural expectations, taboos, and obligations)

These elements replace or override the corresponding assumptions of the generic cleric where appropriate.

#### Requirements

All priests must have a Wisdom score of at least 9. Beyond this, each mythos may impose additional requirements reflecting its nature, expectations, or initiatory structure. These may include minimum ability scores, alignment restrictions, or behavioural prerequisites.

A war deity may require strength and endurance. A principle of healing or insight may require wisdom and empathy. More esoteric or demanding powers may impose stricter conditions still.

#### Arms and Armour

Weapons and armour available to a priest of a specific mythos are defined by that faith.

Some mythoi impose strict limitations on weapon use for symbolic, ritual, or doctrinal reasons. Others grant extensive martial training or demand particular sacred arms. These restrictions are expressions of the faith itself rather than a universal rule of priesthood.

#### Spell Access

Each mythos determines its own access to spheres of influence. These define which forms of divine magic are available to its priests.

A deity or spirit, will grant major or minor access to specific spheres according to its nature and portfolio. This determines the spells available to its priests at each level.

The balance of access across spheres replaces the broader spell access pattern of the cleric class.

#### Granted Powers

In addition to spellcasting, each mythos grants one or more special powers reflecting its nature.

These powers are expressions of the priest’s relationship with the mythos rather than universal clerical abilities. They may enhance combat ability, alter reactions, influence emotions, protect followers, or manifest supernatural effects appropriate to the faith.

There is no fixed list provided from which these powers are selected; each is designed to reflect the identity and role of the mythos in the campaign world.

#### Ethos

All priests of a specific mythos are bound by the expectations of their faith. This includes behavioural requirements, taboos, obligations, and codes of conduct.

Ethos defines how the priest must act in order to maintain their connection to the mythos. Failure to uphold these expectations may result in loss or restriction of powers at the DM’s discretion.

Ethos replaces the general behavioural assumptions of clerics, and is specific to each faith.

#### Design Considerations

Priests of a specific mythos should be balanced holistically against clerics and other priesthoods in the campaign.

A mythos that grants broad weapon access or powerful granted abilities should typically impose greater restrictions on spell access, ethos, or other aspects of the class. Conversely, highly restrictive mythoi may grant stronger divine abilities to compensate.

The goal is not uniformity, but parity of overall capability expressed through different forms of limitation and advantage.

Two appendices provide sample faiths that may be used directly or which DMs may use as inspiration. The first presents generalized faiths associated with broad divine portfolios such as agriculture, death, magic, protection, or weather. The second provides examples drawn from the traditional pantheons of the demihuman peoples.

### Shaman

**Ability Requirements:** Dexterity 9, Constitution 12, Wisdom 12 (in addition to requirements of selected faith)

**Prime Requisites:** Constitution, Wisdom

**Alignment:** per selected faith

**Races Allowed:** per selected faith

Shamans serve as intermediaries between the mortal world and the spirit realm, maintaining relationships with ancestral spirits, nature spirits, and the unseen powers that surround their people. Rather than serving an organized temple hierarchy, a shaman guides and protects a tribe or community through ritual, wisdom, and spiritual communion.

Most shamans originate from tribal, nomadic, or otherwise non-urban cultures where reverence for spirits forms an integral part of daily life. While members of any race may become shamans where such traditions exist, the class is generally inappropriate for characters raised within large settled societies or formal religious institutions. A shaman adventuring in more civilized lands is often viewed as an outsider whose customs and beliefs differ markedly from those of established priesthoods.

Like other priests, a shaman receives divine spells through devotion to a deity, pantheon, or sacred tradition. Their distinctive powers, however, arise from personal relationships with ancestral spirits, animal totems, and spirits of place, cultivated through ritual, obligation, and mutual respect.

**Movement:** A shaman's base movement rate is increased by 3.

**Armour Allowed:** Shamans ignore the armour requirements and restrictions of their faith. They may wear only padded, leather, studded leather, or hide armour. Shield use is however determined by the faith they serve.

**Weapons Allowed:** Shamans may not choose proficiency in Military weapons, even if permitted by their faith, unless proficiency with a particular weapon is an explicit requirement of that faith. They may never choose proficiency in crossbows or the mancatcher. In addition, regardless of the requirements or restrictions of their faith, shamans may always choose proficiency in the axe, club, dart, harpoon, quarterstaff, sling, and spear.

**Talisman:** Shamans use a sacred talisman, fetish, or similar token in place of the holy symbol(s) normally associated with a given faith.

**Sphere Access:** A shaman's sphere access is derived from the priesthood of the same faith. A shaman has the same degree of access (major or minor) as the faith's priest to the spheres of **All, Animal, Combat, Divination, Healing,** and **Plant**. If the faith's priest has access (major or minor) to **Charm, Elemental, Necromantic, Protection, Sun,** or **Weather**, the shaman instead gains **minor access** to that sphere. The shaman has no access to other spheres unless specifically granted to Shaman by the faith description or a Shaman Character Kit.

**Turning or Commanding Undead:** If priests of the shaman's faith can turn or command undead, the shaman gains the same ability but resolves all turning attempts as a priest two levels lower.

**Spirit Magic:** Every shaman begins play having already established a bond with a single minor spirit. As they gain experience they may seek additional spirit allies, broadening the knowledge, perspectives, and supernatural aid available through the spirits with whom they have forged bonds. The rules governing spirit allies, calling spirits, and spirit favours are presented under **Spirit Magic**.

**Strongholds:** Shamans do not establish temples or strongholds. Their authority derives from their relationship with the spirit world rather than temporal power. A shaman who acquires land may establish a shrine, sacred grove, or similar place of worship appropriate to their faith, but gains no special class benefits from doing so.

At 9th level, a shaman instead attracts a small number of apprentices, spirit-seekers, or tribal petitioners as determined by the Dungeon Master.

## Wizards

Wizards are practitioners of arcane magic who manipulate the hidden structures of reality through study, discipline, and force of intellect. Unlike priests, who invoke the ordered principles of the warp through faith and divine authority, wizards shape the mutable substance of the weft directly, bending magical energies to produce extraordinary effects.

Arcane magic is an exacting discipline rather than an innate gift. Every spell is the product of painstaking study, careful experimentation, and years of accumulated knowledge. Wizards record their discoveries in spellbooks, mastering intricate magical formulae that allow them to prepare and cast spells with precision. Their art rewards scholarship and ingenuity, but mistakes can be catastrophic, for the forces they command are neither forgiving nor entirely understood.

The practice of wizardry encompasses many traditions and methods of thought. Some wizards pursue a broad understanding of the Art, while others devote themselves to a single school or discipline, sacrificing versatility in exchange for greater mastery. The nature of arcane magic, the Weave, and the various traditions of wizardry are described in detail in Chapter 13: _Magic_.

#### Arcane Magic

A wizard's magical knowledge is not fixed. Unlike priests, who receive access to spells through divine authority, wizards continually expand their repertoire by discovering, researching, trading, purchasing, or otherwise acquiring new spells throughout their careers.

Every spell must first be learned and recorded before it can be prepared and cast. As a result, a wizard's power depends not only upon experience, but upon the breadth of magical knowledge contained within their spellbook.

#### Spellbooks

A wizard's spellbooks are the product of years of study, containing the formulae, diagrams, mnemonic devices, and personal notation through which its owner understands arcane magic. Although two wizards may know the same spell, their spellbooks seldom resemble one another, each reflecting its creator's habits, theories, and methods of thought.

A wizard can prepare only spells recorded in their own spellbooks. At 1st level, a wizard begins play with a spellbook containing a number of spells determined during character creation. As they advance, additional spells may be discovered, researched, or copied from other spellbooks, gradually expanding the wizard's repertoire.

Spellbooks are among a wizard's most valuable possessions. While they can be replaced if lost or destroyed, recreating years of accumulated knowledge is an expensive and time-consuming undertaking. The rules for learning, copying, researching, and preparing spells are presented in the **Magic** chapter.

#### Learning Spells

Wizards do not automatically gain new spells when advancing in level. Arcane knowledge must instead be discovered, traded, purchased, researched, copied, stolen, or otherwise acquired during play.

When a wizard encounters a spell they do not already know, they may attempt to learn it by studying and deciphering its magical structure. Success depends upon the character's Intelligence.

Once mastered, the spell may be recorded in the wizard's spellbook and prepared normally thereafter. The complete rules for learning and copying spells are presented in Magic.

#### Magical Research

Through experimentation, calculation, and prolonged study, wizards may:

- create new spells,
    
- modify existing spells,
    
- develop magical theories,
    
- analyze supernatural phenomena,
    
- and discover previously unknown magical principles.

Such research is expensive, time-consuming, and potentially dangerous. Failed experiments may produce unstable magical effects, destructive surges, planar disturbances, curses, or unintended transformations.

The exact results and risks of magical research are determined by the DM.

#### Magical Item Creation

Wizards are the foremost creators of permanent magical items. Through arcane formulae and specialized rituals, they may bind supernatural forces into physical objects, granting them enduring magical properties.

At higher levels, wizards may create scrolls, potions, enchanted weapons, protective devices, rings, rods, wands, staves, and many other magical objects, provided they possess the necessary knowledge, spells, materials, facilities, and time.

Even magical items intended primarily for use by other classes often depend upon arcane construction techniques during their creation. Priests may sanctify holy relics, druids may attune sacred natural objects, but the stabilization and binding of enduring magical structure usually falls within the domain of wizardly craft.

#### Arms and Armour

Wizards receive little martial training and may normally gain proficiency only in **Simple** weapons. However, unlike most classes, they are not restricted to non-exotic weapons and may learn exotic simple weapons where appropriate to their tradition, studies, or background.

Wizards normally cannot cast arcane spells while wearing armour. Arcane spellcasting demands exact movement, breath control, symbolic gesture, and uninterrupted energetic shaping; armour interferes with the precision required to manipulate arcane structures safely.

This limitation reflects practical and technical constraints rather than any inherent incompatibility between metal and magic.

#### Hit Dice and Constitution Bonuses

Wizards gain 1d4 hit points per level from 1st through 10th level. Constitution bonuses apply normally during these levels.

Beginning at 11th level, wizards no longer gain additional Hit Dice. Instead, they gain a flat 1 hit point per level thereafter, and no further Constitution bonuses are applied.

### Mage

**Ability Requirements:** **Intelligence 9**

**Prime Requisites:** Intelligence

Mages are the most versatile practitioners of arcane magic, pursuing no single doctrine to the exclusion of others. Where specialists devote themselves to one particular understanding of magical substance, the mage studies the broad interplay of the Art as a whole. This breadth grants flexibility and adaptability, though never the depth of mastery attained by a true specialist.

Arcane magic concerns itself with the manipulation of **substance**: elemental force, material transformation, sensation, thought, life, death, motion, and the innumerable hidden properties underlying the manifest world. To the mage, the multiverse is not a fixed creation, but mutable actuality—matter and energy awaiting manipulation through knowledge, will, and disciplined practice.

Because their studies range widely across many magical disciplines, mages are uniquely suited to confronting unfamiliar circumstances. A specialist may wield terrifying command within his chosen field yet remain dangerously limited outside it; the mage, though less refined in any single doctrine, possesses a broader command of the Art and is therefore more adaptable in the uncertain conditions of adventuring life.

This versatility comes at a cost. The mage cannot achieve the same intuitive grasp of any single discipline as a specialist wizard. The subtle resonances, hidden sympathies, and perfected techniques known to dedicated specialists remain beyond the mage’s reach. Thus specialists learn, research, and employ the magic of their chosen disciplines with greater ease and potency.

Mages occupy many roles within society. Some pursue knowledge in great libraries and academies, while others live as reclusive hermits, itinerant scholars, court advisers, physicians, astrologers, or alchemists. Whatever their station, they share a belief that the hidden structures of the world can be understood through disciplined study.

Mages are the purest expression of wizards and have no additional rules, requirements, benefits, or hindrances beyond what is defined in the Wizard group description.

### Specialist Wizards

Specialist wizards devote themselves to a single school of arcane study. Rather than seeking broad understanding of the Weave, they pursue mastery through depth, refining their knowledge of one magical tradition at the expense of others. This focus grants exceptional command within their chosen discipline, but inevitably limits their understanding of competing approaches to arcane magic.

Specialist wizards gain significant advantages within their chosen discipline, but these are balanced by corresponding limitations. Their mastery allows them to cast, learn, and research the magic of their specialty more effectively than a mage, while restricting or preventing access to incompatible schools of arcane study.

As a specialist's understanding deepens, it becomes increasingly shaped by the assumptions and methods of a single magical tradition. Other schools, particularly those founded upon opposing principles, become progressively more difficult to comprehend until they can no longer be practised safely. This limitation reflects the specialist's worldview as much as any technical inability.

Not all wizards can become specialists. The player character must meet certain requirements to become a specialist. Most specialist wizards must be single-classed; with few exceptions multi-classed characters cannot become specialists. Dual-class humans _can_ choose to become specialists. The dedication to a particular school of magic requires all the attention and concentration of the character. Such dedication demands complete concentration, leaving little opportunity for the training required by another class.

In addition, each school has different restrictions on race, ability scores, and schools of magic allowed. These restrictions are given in the table below. Note that universal magic is not available as a specialty. The spells of this group, vital to the functioning of a wizard, are available to all wizards.

Specialists cannot learn spells belonging to their opposition schools, nor use magical items that duplicate spells from those schools. Elementalists are an exception: their restrictions derive not from philosophical incompatibility, but from the natural opposition of the elements themselves, with fire opposing water and earth opposing air.

#### Specialist Wizard Benefits

As described under the Wizard group benefits, Specialists receive an increased Spell Point progression and may cast one additional spell of each spell level per day, provided at least one spell cast at that level belongs to their specialty.

Unless otherwise stated a Specialists receive a bonus of +15% when learning spells from their school and a penalty of -15% when learning spells from other schools. The bonus or penalty is applied to the percentile dice roll the player must make when the character tries to learn a new spell (see Table 4: Intelligence).

Whenever a specialist reaches a new spell level, he automatically gains one spell of his school to add to his spell books. No roll for learning the spell need be made. It is assumed that the character has discovered this new spell during the course of his research and study.

When determining the difficulty of researching a new spell within the specialist's school, treat the spell as one level lower than its actual level. An enchanter attempting to create a new enchantment spell would have an easier time of it than an illusionist attempting to do the same.

### Philosophical Specialists

#### Abjurer

Abjurers study the principles of magical stability, protection, containment, and resistance. Where other wizards seek to alter the substance of the Weave, abjurers concern themselves with preserving structure, defining boundaries, and preventing unwanted change. Their art governs wards, barriers, banishment, dispelling, and the suppression of hostile magic.

To an abjurer, the multiverse is sustained not only by creation and transformation, but by the limits that prevent those forces from descending into chaos. Every barrier - physical, magical, or conceptual - represents an expression of ordered structure imposed upon the mutable substance of reality.

The discipline demands patience, restraint, and sound judgment. A moment's carelessness may leave a ward incomplete or a protective working fatally compromised. For this reason, successful abjurers possess exceptional self-control and disciplined thought, qualities reflected in the Wisdom required to master the school.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents modify their saving throws by -1 when attempting to save against an abjuration spell cast by an abjurer. An abjurer adds a +1 bonus when saving against abjuration spells.

**Acquired Powers:**  

At **8th level**, an abjurer gains a +1 bonus to saving throws against paralyzation, poison, and death magic.

At **11th level**, his mastery of protective and stabilizing magic has become so powerful that his Base Armour Class improves by 1.

At **14th level**, the abjurer gains immunity to all forms of _hold_ spells

#### Conjurer

Conjurers study the creation, calling, and transference of substance across the boundaries of reality. Their art encompasses the summoning of creatures, the creation of temporary matter, the opening of pathways between distant places, and the manipulation of the unseen connections linking the planes of existence.

To a conjurer, distance and separation are not immutable facts, but conditions that may be overcome through sufficient understanding of the Weave. Matter may be called where none previously existed, creatures may answer bindings laid across the planes, and places far apart may be brought into temporary contact. Every successful conjuration demonstrates that the divisions mortals perceive between worlds are neither absolute nor permanent.

The discipline demands endurance, concentration, and unwavering force of will. Errors in a conjuration may produce unstable manifestations, uncontrolled entities, or breaches through which unwanted forces enter the world. Successful conjurers possess exceptional stamina, reflected in the Constitution required to master the school.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents modify their saving throws by -1 when attempting to save against a conjuration or summoning spell cast by a conjurer. A conjurer adds a +1 bonus when saving against conjuration spells or against attacks from creatures or forces created by conjuration or summoning magic.

**Acquired Powers:**  
At **11th level**, a conjurer no longer requires material components when casting spells from the school of conjuration or summoning.

At **14th level**, a conjurer may instantly dispel creatures created by a _monster summoning_ spell, or an equivalent effect. Up to 10 Hit Dice of creatures may be affected with each use, though no creature with more than 5 Hit Dice can be dispelled in this way. The ability may be used three times per day by pointing at the affected creatures and concentrating.

#### Diviner

Diviners study the hidden patterns by which knowledge is revealed. Their art encompasses perception beyond the ordinary senses, the discovery of concealed truths, the interpretation of omens, and the observation of distant places, forgotten events, and possible futures.

To a diviner, the Weave contains far more information than mortal minds ordinarily perceive. Past and present leave enduring impressions upon reality, while the future exists as a shifting tapestry of possibilities rather than fixed destiny. Divination does not create knowledge, but uncovers what already exists, allowing the wizard to perceive truths hidden by distance, time, deception, or ignorance.

The discipline demands patience, insight, and careful judgment. A diviner must distinguish genuine revelation from misleading possibility, symbolic vision, or deliberate magical deception. Successful diviners possess exceptional perception and strength of mind, qualities reflected in the Wisdom required to master the school.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents modify their saving throws by -1 when attempting to save against a divination spell cast by a diviner. A diviner gains a +1 bonus when saving against divination spells or magical effects that duplicate them.

**Acquired Powers:**

At **11th level**, a diviner may cast the 2nd-level priest spell _find traps_ three times per day without verbal components and a casting time of 1 round.

At **14th level**, a diviner becomes immune to magical scrying spells. Spells such as _ESP_ and _know alignment_, _clairaudience_ or similar magic automatically fail and give the caster no response.

#### Enchanter

Enchanters study the manipulation of thought, emotion, perception, and will. Their art governs the shaping of desire, the alteration of mood, the imposition of suggestion, and the subtle binding of one mind to another. Through enchantment, the wizard does not alter the substance of the world, but the way it is perceived and responded to by living minds.

To an enchanter, consciousness is not fixed or wholly self-determined, but a fluid expression of underlying arcane patterns. Thoughts, fears, loyalties, and desires are subject to influence and reshaping through precise interaction with the Weave. The boundary between persuasion and compulsion is not always clear, and enchantment explores that boundary directly.

The discipline demands empathy, perception, and careful control. Misjudgment in enchantment risks backlash, resistance, or unintended alteration of personality and intent. Successful enchanters possess exceptional force of personality, reflected in the Charisma required to master the school.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents modify their saving throws by -1 when attempting to save against an enchantment/charm spell cast by an enchanter. An enchanter gains a +1 bonus when saving against enchantment/charm spells.

**Acquired Powers:**  
At **11th Level**, Once per day, the enchanter may cast _free action_ as a spell-like ability requiring no material components. Casting time is 1 and duration is 1 hour. The ability otherwise functions as the 4th-level priest spell.

At **14th Level**, the enchanter becomes immune to all _charm_ effects that would apply to his current form (including _charm person_, _charm monster_, and similar magical compulsion effects).

#### Illusionist

Illusionists study the manipulation of perception, sensation, and the interpretation of reality within the minds of observers. Their art governs the creation of false images, altered sounds, sensory distortions, and constructed experiences that exist only through the interaction between the Weave and conscious perception. Illusion does not alter substance directly, but reshapes the way substance is perceived.

To an illusionist, reality is inseparable from observation. What is experienced is not necessarily what is, and what is “true” depends upon the limits and reliability of perception. Illusion magic exploits this uncertainty, constructing coherent sensory frameworks that may mislead, conceal, or overwhelm the senses of those exposed to it.

The discipline demands precision, imagination, and an intuitive understanding of perception. Illusions must be internally consistent to remain convincing; inconsistencies risk immediate collapse of the effect. Successful illusionists possess exceptional dexterity, reflecting the fine control required to shape complex sensory patterns.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** Illusionists add a +1 bonus when making saving throws against illusion spells. Opponents suffer a -1 penalty when making saving throws against illusion/phantasm spells cast by an illusionist.

**Acquired Powers:**  
At **8th level**, an illusionist gains a +1 bonus on saving throws against illusions cast by non-illusionists. This stacks with their existing resistance, for a total bonus of +2 against such effects.

At **11th level**, an illusionist may cast a special _dispel illusion_ up to three times per day. This ability has a range of 30 yards and has a casting time of 1. No components are required, only the ability to point at the target. The illusionist may target any _phantasmal force_ or equivalent illusion _not_ created by another illusionist. The base chance of success is 50%, modified by 5% per level difference between the illusionist and the original caster. If successful, the illusion is immediately dispelled. If used against a non-illusory effect, the attempt has no effect but still expends one daily use.

> It's not clear why elves and half-elves are unable to specialize in this school, though some speculate that the same inborn magical resistance that helps make them immune to charm spells also limits their ability to focus the magical energy needed to create illusions.

#### Invoker

Invokers study the direct manipulation of raw arcane force. Their art concerns the shaping of energy itself into destructive, transformative, or controlling effects: flame, lightning, force, sound, and the many expressions of power that act upon the world without intermediary form. Invocation does not conceal or redirect reality, but imposes arcane energy upon it with overwhelming immediacy.

To an invoker, the Weave is not subtle or interpretive but immediate and coercive—a substrate through which power may be expressed with minimal abstraction. Magic is not suggestion or transformation, but direct application of force shaped by will and precision. The discipline is therefore defined by intensity, clarity of purpose, and control under pressure.

The discipline demands resilience, focus, and exceptional endurance. The sustained channelling of raw arcane energy places significant strain upon both body and mind, and misjudgment in its application can result in catastrophic feedback or uncontrolled release. Successful invokers possess exceptional Constitution, reflecting their ability to withstand this burden.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents suffer a -1 penalty when making saving throws against invocation/evocation spells cast by an invoker. An invoker adds a +1 bonus when saving against invocation/evocation spells and magical items that simulate these spells.

**Acquired Powers:**

At **8th level**, an invoker's saving throw modifiers against invocation/evocation spells increase to +2, and at **11th level** to +3.

At **14th level**, an invoker becomes immune to one invocation/evocation spell or 3rd level or lower (but not magical items or breath weapons that replicate that spell)

#### Necromancer

Necromancers study the boundary between life and death, and the flow of vitality that defines both. Their magic concerns decay, mortality, negative energy, and the manipulation of life force—whether weakening the living, restoring what has been lost, or binding the dead to service. No other school confronts the nature of existence so directly or so uncomfortably.

To the necromancer, life and death are not moral absolutes but states of energetic transition within the Weave. Life is a temporary expression of ordered vitality; death is not an ending but a transformation into different patterns of energy and entropy. Their magic operates at this boundary, and constant exposure to such forces often shapes both perception and temperament.

The discipline demands emotional resilience, intellectual discipline, and a willingness to confront concepts that most others avoid. Necromancy is not inherently corrupt, but it is isolating: those who practice it must endure fear, revulsion, and misunderstanding, while maintaining control over forces that easily overwhelm weaker wills. A strong Wisdom is required to master the school safely.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents suffer a -1 penalty when making saving throws against necromancy spells cast by a necromancer. A necromancer adds a +1 bonus when saving against necromancy spells.

**Acquired Powers:**  

At **8th level**, a necromancer gains an additional +1 bonus when saving against necromancy spells and against attacks made by undead creatures.

At **11th level**, the necromancer may use _speak with dead_ as a spell-like ability requiring no verbal or material components. He need only point at a corpse and concentrate for one round. The ability functions as the 3rd-level priest spell, except the necromancer may converse for up to one turn and ask up to four questions. No saving throw is permitted. This ability may be used only once per corpse.

At **14th level**, a necromancer gains a +2 bonus to saving throws against undead special attacks that produce ongoing effects (including strength drain and paralysis). In addition, he may attempt a saving throw vs. death magic at a –4 penalty to resist effects that normally allow no save (such as energy drain from wights or wraiths). On a successful save, the special effect is negated, though any normal damage still applies.

#### Transmuter

Transmuters study the alteration of substance: the reshaping, recombination, and reconfiguration of matter, energy, and living form. Their magic concerns transformation in its most practical sense, whether subtle adjustments to physical properties or dramatic changes that rewrite the nature of a target entirely.

To a transmuter, reality is a system of interacting components rather than fixed truths. Everything can be adjusted, refined, or rebuilt given sufficient understanding of its underlying structure. Their perspective is analytical and mechanical: even living creatures are complex systems governed by rules that can, in principle, be understood and modified.

The discipline demands precision, patience, and careful observation. Alteration magic is highly sensitive to error, and even small miscalculations can produce unstable or unintended results. Successful transmuters possess strong Dexterity and a disciplined, methodical mindset that allows them to control delicate and intricate magical processes.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents suffer a -1 penalty when making saving throws against alteration spells cast by a transmuter. A transmuter adds a +1 bonus when saving against alteration spells cast by others.

**Acquired Powers:**  

At **8th level**, a transmuter's saving throw modifiers increase to 2, and at **11th level** to 3.

### Effectual Specialists

#### Dimensionalist

Dimensionalists study the manipulation of space, time, and extradimensional structure. Their magic concerns the boundaries between realities: pockets of folded space, alternate planes, temporal distortion, and the hidden geometry that underlies movement and distance. They are students of transit, containment, and dislocation, operating at the edges of what reality permits.

To a dimensionalist, the Weave is not only substance but structure: a layered continuum of connected spaces and hidden interfaces between worlds. Spells are understood as controlled breaches, folds, or reconfigurations of spatial law, allowing access to otherwise unreachable positions or states of existence. Magic is therefore a matter of geometry, positioning, and dimensional logic rather than elemental force or transformation.

The discipline demands exceptional intellect, spatial reasoning, and abstract comprehension. Dimensional magic relies on concepts that defy intuitive understanding, and errors in interpretation can result in dangerous displacement or loss of access to reality. Only those with strong Intelligence can reliably grasp and manipulate these structures.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents suffer a -1 penalty when making saving throws against dimensionalist spells. A dimensionalist gains a +1 bonus when saving against dimensional effects and spells.

**Acquired Powers:**  

At **8th level**, a dimensionalist gains the ability to _disappear_ by stepping into a personal pocket dimension once per day. While within this extradimensional space, the dimensionalist is immune to all attacks and external effects and can only be detected by magic that reveals dimensional openings. However, the dimensionalist has no awareness of events in the world left behind.

The dimensionalist may remain within the pocket dimension for up to one hour. During this time, they may perform personal actions such as resting, reading, or consuming a potion, but cannot affect or interact with the outside world. The point of exit is always the exact location at which the dimensionalist entered the dimension, and no magic (including _teleport_, _dimension door_, _shadow walk_, or similar effects) functions to allow escape from within the pocket dimension.

At **11th level**, the dimensionalist may bring one other human-sized creature or an object weighing up to 500 pounds into the pocket dimension.

At **14th level**, the dimensionalist may bring up to five companions or a total of 1,500 pounds of non-living matter into the pocket dimension.

#### Elementalist

Elementalists study arcane magic through the lens of the four classical elements: air, earth, fire, and water. Rather than interpreting magic through the philosophical schools, they reduce arcane structure to elemental forces, treating all spells as expressions of elemental affinity or non-elemental interference. Their practice is defined by alignment with a chosen element and rejection of its natural opposition.

To an elementalist, the Weave is fundamentally material and environmental. Magic is not an abstract system of competing theories, but the direct expression of elemental forces that permeate existence. Fire, air, earth, and water are not symbolic categories but active principles through which arcane power is shaped and expressed in the world.

The discipline demands attunement, instinct, and disciplined focus. Elemental magic is intensely specialized and often volatile, requiring the wizard to maintain absolute control over a narrow domain of power while remaining unable to safely access its opposing force. Strong will and perceptive control are essential to prevent elemental imbalance.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents suffer a -2 penalty when making saving throws against spells of the elementalist’s specialty element. The elementalist gains a +2 bonus when saving against spells of their specialty element.

**Acquired Powers:**  

Elementalists follow the normal rules for specialist wizards, except as follows:

All references to “school” in specialist rules are replaced with the elementalist’s chosen element.

The normal specialist Learn Spells modifiers are replaced with the following:

- +25% for spells of the elementalist’s chosen element
    
- +15% for non-opposed elemental spells
    
- –25% for non-elemental spells
    
- The elementalist cannot learn or cast spells of the opposing element

Once per day, the elementalist may cast one spell of their chosen element as if they were **1d4 levels higher**, modifying range, duration, area of effect, and damage accordingly.

At **11th Level** the elementalist need not concentrate to control an elemental of their chosen type summoned via _conjure elemental_.

At **14th Level** elementals of the elementalist's chosen type summoned with _conjure elemental_ can no longer turn on the caster.

#### Force Mage

Force mages study the manipulation of raw magical force: coherent arcane energy shaped into force-fields, barriers, impacts, and construct-like expressions of power. Their magic is neither subtle nor indirect; it is the application of structured force that mimics solidity, pressure, and directed kinetic effect.

To a force mage, the Weave is best understood as a reservoir of pure energetic pressure that can be condensed and shaped into stable forms. Magic is treated as a physical analogue—something that can exert force, resist intrusion, or impose movement and constraint upon matter. Spells are therefore exercises in containment and projection rather than transformation or illusion.

The discipline demands strength of will, endurance, and precise control under strain. Force magic is taxing and difficult to stabilize, requiring both intellectual discipline and physical resilience to prevent backlash or collapse of constructed effects. A strong Intelligence and Constitution are required to master this school.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** All opponents suffer a -1 penalty when making saving throws against force spells cast by a force mage. A force mage gains a +1 bonus when saving against force spells and effects.

**Acquired Powers:**  

At **8th level**, a force mage’s saving throw bonus against force spells and effects increases by +1, for a total bonus of +2.

At **11th level**, the force mage may attempt a saving throw for half damage against any force-based spell that inflicts damage, even if the spell does not normally allow a saving throw.

At **14th level**, the force mage’s saving throw bonus against magical force increases to +3.

#### Shadow Mage

Shadow mages study the arcane nature of darkness, twilight, and obscured perception. Their magic is rooted in the belief that all shadows are metaphysically linked through the Demiplane of Shadow, forming a hidden network through which perception, concealment, and influence can be extended. Their spells draw upon this connection to manipulate visibility, misdirection, and the boundary between seen and unseen.

To a shadow mage, the Weave is partially concealed—an underlayer of reality that becomes accessible through darkness and absence of light. Shadow is not merely the negation of illumination, but a distinct medium of arcane interaction, capable of carrying influence, shaping perception, and bridging the material world with deeper, obscured planes of existence.

The discipline demands keen perception, mental discipline, and emotional resilience. Shadow magic relies on subtle interpretation of light and absence, requiring precise control over shifting conditions and an ability to operate without reliable sensory certainty. Strong Intelligence and Wisdom are required to master this school safely.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** Opponents’ saving throw modifiers against a shadow mage vary depending on lighting conditions, ranging from +2 in bright light to −4 in deep shadow (see table below). The shadow mage gains no saving throw modifier against any spells.

| Lighting Conditions              | Modifier |
| -------------------------------- | -------- |
| Bright daylight/continual light  | +2       |
| Weak daylight/dusk/light         | none     |
| Twilight/moonlight/lantern light | -1       |
| Weak moonlight/torch light       | -2       |
| Candlelight/starlight            | -3       |
| Total darkness                   | -4       |
### Thaumaturgical Specialists

#### Alchemist

Alchemists study the transformation of matter through elemental principles, treating all substances as combinations and recombinations of air, earth, fire, and water. Their magic and craft blur the boundary between science and sorcery, as they seek to understand, isolate, and reproduce the underlying properties that govern material change. Their focus is experimental, empirical, and relentlessly inquisitive.

To an alchemist, the Weave is expressed through physical substance and its mutable composition. Reality is not shaped primarily through force or symbolism, but through the rearrangement of elemental building blocks that define all material things. Magic is therefore a process of analysis and reconstruction, where understanding precedes manipulation and precision determines outcome.

The discipline demands patience, accuracy, and disciplined experimentation. Alchemy tolerates no imprecision; small errors in measurement or formulation can produce unpredictable or dangerous results. Successful alchemists possess exceptional Intelligence and Dexterity, reflecting both their analytical capacity and their steady control in practical application.

**Special Requirement:** An alchemist requires access to a suitable laboratory. Without one, he cannot benefit from his specialist bonus spell, conduct magical research, brew alchemical potions, or add new spells to his spellbook.

An alchemist begins play with a laboratory worth 1,000 gp and must expand and improve it as he advances, investing 1,000 gp per character level in additions and improvements. Maintaining a laboratory costs 50 gp per character level each month.

An alchemist may also maintain a field laboratory while travelling. A field laboratory allows him to retain his specialist bonus spell and add new spells to his spellbook, although all rolls to learn spells made using a field laboratory suffer a –10% penalty. A field laboratory cannot be used for magical research or alchemical potion brewing.

See **Laboratories, Libraries, and Workshops** in the _Structures and Strongholds_ appendix.

**Acquired Powers:**

Spells belonging to the school of alchemy are treated as having **no verbal component** when cast by an alchemist, as their effects are produced through prepared reagents rather than spoken incantations.

At **6th level**, an alchemist gains the ability to create potions through alchemical methods rather than conventional magical item creation.

Before brewing a potion, the alchemist must first research its formula. Formula research functions similarly to spell research, treating the potion's effective spell level as its XP value divided by 100 (round fractions up). Research requires two weeks and 500 gp per effective spell level. The alchemist must successfully make a Learn Spells roll to master the formula. The number of formulae the alchemist may know is limited by the maximum number of spells per level permitted by his Intelligence.

Once a formula has been mastered, the alchemist may brew a dose by spending one uninterrupted week in a suitable laboratory and investing **3d6 × 100 gp** in materials. A second Learn Spells roll is required to execute the formula successfully, with a **+1% bonus per character level**. Unlike conventional potion creation, alchemical brewing requires no rare magical ingredients, although unusual chemicals, apparatus, or other mundane materials may still need to be obtained.

#### Artificer

Artificers study the manipulation, containment, and redirection of magical energy through physical form. Where most wizards work directly upon the Weave, artificers impose structure upon it through crafted intermediaries—objects, vessels, and arcane frameworks designed to store or channel spellcraft. Their magic is not merely cast, but engineered.

To an artificer, magic is not an ephemeral force but a substance that can be measured, shaped, and retained. Every spell has weight, cost, and configuration; every enchantment is a problem of design. In this view, the multiverse is a system of energies that can be stabilized through correct construction, allowing power to be held rather than simply released.

Mastering this discipline requires patience, precision, and an unusually methodical intellect. Artificers must be able to think in terms of structure and consequence rather than impulse, and they rely heavily on careful planning and disciplined execution. Their work demands both technical intelligence and the endurance to sustain long, meticulous processes.

**Special Requirement:** An artificer requires access to a suitable laboratory. Without one, he cannot benefit from his specialist bonus spell, conduct magical research, create magical items, or add new spells to his spellbook.

An artificer begins play with a laboratory worth 1000 gp and must expand and improve it as he advances, investing 1,000 gp per character level in additions and improvements. Maintaining a laboratory costs 50 gp per character level each month.

An artificer may also maintain a field laboratory while travelling. A field laboratory allows him to retain his specialist bonus spell and add new spells to his spellbook, although all rolls to learn spells made using a field laboratory suffer a –10% penalty. A field laboratory cannot be used for magical research or magical item creation.

See **Laboratories, Libraries, and Workshops** in the _Structures and Strongholds_ appendix.

**Acquired Powers:**  

By examining a magical item for one uninterrupted turn, an artificer has a **20% chance at 1st level** to determine its general purpose and function. This chance increases by **5% per level** thereafter.

**At 4th level:** The artificer may prepare specially crafted items capable of storing spells he knows. Preparing a vessel requires one uninterrupted week of work, followed by one day to bind a spell into the item at a cost of 500 gp per spell level. The base item must be of exceptional workmanship and worth at least 100 gp.

Each prepared item stores a single spell indefinitely until discharged. Only the artificer may activate the stored spell, which is released with a casting time of 1 and otherwise functions exactly as though cast normally. Once discharged, the item may be prepared again.

The artificer may have a total number of stored spell levels equal to his character level at any one time.

**At 7th level:** The artificer learns to create temporary magical items for his own use. Any permanent magical item not specifically restricted to another class may be researched and reproduced in temporary form.

Research requires one week per 500 XP value of the item, at a minimum cost of 100 gp per week, followed by a successful learn spells check. Possessing an example of the item, or obtaining detailed knowledge through spells such as _legend lore_ or _contact other plane_, halves the required research time.

Once researched, creating a temporary item requires half the research time, materials costing 2d6 × 100 gp (plus the cost of the physical item), and a second successful learn spells check.

Temporary items function only for their creator and remain magical for **1d6 days plus one day per artificer level**. Charged items begin with one charge per artificer level. Once the enchantment expires, the item may be re-enchanted with one uninterrupted week of work, another expenditure of 2d6 × 100 gp, and a successful learn spells check.

**Note:** Artificers create permanent magical items using the normal magical item creation rules. If they have previously researched a temporary version of the same item, any later permanent creation uses the minimum research time and expense normally required. Artificers gain a **+10% bonus** to all chances to successfully enchant permanent magical items.

#### Geometer

Geometers study the structure of magic as a system of symbols, spatial relations, and precise arcane notation. Rather than treating spells as fluid expressions of will, they reduce them to diagrams of force, inscribed patterns, and geometric relationships that encode magical effects. Their work lies at the intersection of mathematics, linguistics, and arcane theory, where correctness of form is inseparable from correctness of effect.

To a geometer, the Weave is not a living or reactive substance but a structured field that can be mapped, measured, and replicated through perfect notation. Spells are not “cast” so much as _solved_—the correct arrangement of symbols, proportions, and spatial constraints produces predictable arcane outcomes. In this worldview, error is not misfortune but distortion: a flaw in structure that must be eliminated through refinement.

Mastery of this discipline requires exceptional intellect, patience, and obsessive attention to detail. The geometer must be able to hold complex symbolic systems in mind and reproduce them with absolute precision, since even minor deviation can result in failure or unpredictable magical consequences. This is a school for methodical thinkers who prefer certainty of structure over improvisation.

**Acquired Powers:**

All spells of the school of geometry are cast without verbal components, relying instead on symbolic construction and structured manipulation of arcane form.

**At 4th level:** The geometer may transcribe spells he knows into scroll form. These scrolls may later be activated by reading them (requiring one full round), after which the scroll is consumed. The geometer may maintain a number of prepared spell-scrolls equal to his character level.

Creating a spell-scroll requires one full day per spell level and costs 100 gp per spell level in materials. Only spells of the school of geometry may normally be transcribed, though the geometer may engage in spell research to learn how to transcribe other spells they know. Regardless of school, a successful learn spells check is required to complete transcription.

**At 7th level:** The geometer may research and construct protective scrolls. Each such scroll must first be individually researched, treating its effective spell level as:

**Effective Level = (XP Value ÷ 500) + 2**

Research requires two weeks per effective level and costs 1,000 gp per level. A successful learn spells check is required.

Once researched, the geometer may create a single scroll in one week of work, at a cost of 3d6 × 100 gp in materials. A second learn spells check is required to successfully complete the item.

Only one protective scroll may be produced at a time per completed research.

#### Song Mage

Song mages study the shaping of magic through voice, rhythm, and structured sound. Their discipline treats melody and speech not as expression, but as precise arcane instrumentation: carefully controlled sequences of pitch, cadence, and resonance that align the Weave into specific magical effects. Where other wizards rely on gestures and formulae, the song mage uses performance as the primary structure of spellcasting.

To a song mage, magic is fundamentally harmonic. The universe is understood as a system that can be influenced through resonance, where sound becomes a bridge between intention and reality. Spells are not imposed upon the world but _brought into alignment_ with it through correct auditory form. In this view, failure is discord—an inability to maintain the necessary coherence of voice and meaning.

Mastering this school requires both intellectual discipline and expressive precision. A song mage must be able to control subtle variations in tone and timing while maintaining mental focus on complex magical structures. This makes the discipline unusually demanding, requiring analytical understanding paired with performance skill and force of personality.

**Saving Throw Modifiers:** Opponents suffer a –1 penalty to saving throws against song spells cast by a song mage. Song mages add +1 to saving throws for spells of the school of song. In addition, they may apply their saving throw bonus against _any_ magical sound- or music-based effects, including both spells (such as _shout_) and creature abilities (such as a sphinx’s roar or banshee’s wail), as well as magical items that produce sonic effects.

**Acquired Powers:**  

All spells of the school of song are cast through vocal performance and therefore require no somatic or material components. However, the song mage is highly vulnerable to silence effects, which prevent spellcasting entirely. Provided they can vocalise freely a song mage can cast spells while restrained, bound, or deprived of equipment.

At 8th level, the song mage may enhance the effectiveness of any musical or sound-based magical item by 50%. This increases beneficial effects such as range, area, duration, or potency. If the item produces a harmful or cursed effect, that effect is reduced by 50% where applicable. This ability applies only to effects mediated through sound, music, or sonic vibration.

At 11th level, once per day the song mage may counter a magical sound- or music-based effect within a 10-foot radius by singing in response. This negates the effect for all creatures in the area. The song mage must remain stationary or move slowly and sing for one full round before the effect is negated, and may continue singing for up to one turn per level to sustain protection against ongoing or repeated sonic effects.

#### Wild Mage

### Wild Mage

Wild mages study magic as an expression of instability, probability, and unpredictable interaction between arcane forces. Rather than treating the Weave as a structured system to be understood and controlled, they regard it as a fluctuating field of potential outcomes where certainty is always approximate. Their discipline is built on the premise that randomness is not failure of understanding, but a fundamental property of magical reality.

To a wild mage, spellcasting is not the application of fixed formulas but the release of controlled variance within a system that cannot be fully predicted. Every spell is an experiment in probability, where intent is filtered through unstable arcane conditions. In this view, the multiverse is not governed by strict rules, but by patterns of fluctuation that can be influenced—but never entirely stabilized.

Mastering this discipline requires intellectual resilience, adaptability, and a willingness to operate without certainty. Wild mages must be able to function under conditions where outcomes cannot be guaranteed, maintaining focus despite unpredictable results. This makes the tradition mentally demanding in a fundamentally different way from other schools, rewarding flexibility of thought over precision.

**Acquired Powers:**  
Wild magic introduces controlled instability into the casting process and every spell cast by a wild mage is subject to unpredictable variation in power and outcome. Wild Mages use the following special rules:

##### Wild Magic Aptitude

Wild magic does not follow normal patterns of magical study. The normal benefits of specialist wizards are amended as follows:

- Wild mages gain a +10% bonus when learning wild magic spells.
    
- They suffer a −5% penalty when learning spells from other schools.
    
- When researching wild magic spells, the effective spell level is treated as one level lower for difficulty and cost purposes.   

##### Spell Level Variation

When a wild mage casts a spell, roll 1d20 immediately after the casting is declared (and during combat, before initiative is rolled).

- **1–6:** The spell is weakened. The caster’s effective level is reduced by 1d3 levels (minimum 1st level). This affects all level-dependent variables such as damage, range, duration, area of effect, and any other scaling factors.
    
- **7–9:** The spell functions normally.
    
- **10:** A wild surge occurs (see below).
    
- **11–14:** The spell functions normally.
    
- **15–20:** The spell is empowered. The caster’s effective level is increased by 1d3 levels for all level-dependent effects.

The level variation result may not exceed the caster's own level (i.e. a 2nd level wild mage is limited to a reduction or increase of 2 levels )

The caster's true level is always used when calculating range, duration, area of effect, etc. of surge spells. Regardless of the surge result the original spell is expended unless the DM rules otherwise.

If in the DM's judgment a wild surge result is impossible or would have no effect (for example a surge result of 'target changes sex' occurs while casting _Wizard Lock_ on a door, or a stone is _hastened_) nothing affecting play happens. If the DM decides a surge result would be too destructive to the story they should re-roll the surge effect - the randomness of wild surges should never be allowed to ruin the adventure.

##### Magical Item Instability

Wild mages have an unusual affinity with chaotic magical artifacts. When a wild mage attempts to use one of the following items, they may attempt to control its effect:

- amulet of the planes
    
- bag of beans
    
- bag of tricks
    
- deck of illusions
    
- deck of many things
    
- well of many worlds

The wild mage has a 50% chance to choose the outcome rather than suffer a random result.

The _wand of wonder_ also functions differently. If control is achieved, the wild mage may expend charges to cast any wizard spell they know, whether or not it is memorized. The number of charges expended equals the spell’s level. On a failed control check, the wand activates normally and consumes only one charge.

## Rogue

Rogues survive through adaptability, skill, cunning, and opportunism. Where warriors rely upon strength and priests upon faith, rogues prosper through agility, perception, charm, stealth, and a willingness to go where others will not.

The rogue group encompasses many different callings. Thieves, scouts, spies, burglars, swindlers, performers, confidence artists, explorers, investigators, tomb robbers, assassins, and countless other professions all fall within the broad category of rogue. Some operate openly as respected specialists or entertainers, while others survive at the edges of law, custom, and polite society.

Not all rogues are criminals, though many acquire reputations that invite suspicion. Some are adventurers, scouts, agents, diplomats, couriers, or wandering performers; others are smugglers, cutpurses, fences, or informants. Many rogues drift between legitimate and illicit work as circumstance demands.

Rogues combine talents drawn from many disciplines. Though not as formidable in battle as warriors, nor as magically powerful as priests or wizards, they compensate through versatility and practical skill. Rogues can use a wide variety of weapons, armour, and magical items, though individual classes may impose further restrictions.

The rogue group also includes traditions that rely upon secrecy, espionage, disguise, infiltration, and unconventional methods of conflict. Future supplements or campaign settings may introduce additional rogue classes, such as ninjas, spies, or other specialized operatives.

#### Rogue Skills

All rogues possess specialized skills reflecting agility, awareness, and practical experience.

Most rogues are adept climbers, capable of scaling surfaces that would challenge ordinary men. They are observant and attentive, often noticing faint sounds, hidden movement, or signs of danger overlooked by others. Rogues also tend to possess broad familiarity with languages, codes, dialects, and obscure forms of writing encountered during their travels. The exact skills available depend upon the specific rogue class.

Each class of rogue begins with a different base rogue skill score as given in the following table, which is then subject to modifiers for dexterity, race, character kit, and armour. Individual class descriptions may include additional rules applicable only to the class. The remaining rules for using these skills and the modifiers are found in Chapter 10.

#### Arms and Armour

Rogues favour mobility, concealment, and versatility over heavy protection. Most rogue classes permit a moderate range of weapons and lighter forms of armour, though exact restrictions vary by class.

Certain rogue abilities may function only while wearing light armour or avoiding excessive encumbrance.

#### Magical Items

Rogues are able to use a wider variety of magical items than most characters outside the wizard group. Depending upon class, rogues may employ magical scrolls, potions, rings, enchanted weapons and armour, and certain specialized magical devices.

The exact magical items available depend upon the individual rogue class.

#### Hit Dice and Constitution Bonuses

Rogues gain 1d6 hit points per level from 1st through 10th level. Constitution bonuses apply normally during these levels.

Beginning at 11th level, rogues no longer gain additional Hit Dice. Instead, they gain a flat 2 hit points per level thereafter, and no further Constitution bonuses are applied.

### Thief

**Ability Requirement:** Dex 9

**Prime Requisite:** Dex

**Alignment:** Any

The thief is the archetypal rogue: agile, adaptable, opportunistic, and skilled in the arts of stealth, infiltration, deception, and survival. Thieves thrive where caution, subtlety, and quick reflexes matter more than brute force or open confrontation.

Though often associated with burglary, smuggling, confidence schemes, espionage, or other illicit professions, not all thieves are hardened criminals. Scouts, treasure hunters, spies, investigators, urban guides, and wandering adventurers may all possess the same specialized talents. In many stories and legends, the thief appears as a clever trickster or folk hero: a scoundrel perhaps, but not always a villain.

#### Arms and Armour

Thieves rely upon stealth, precision, and agility, and therefore favour arms and armour that can be worn inconspicuously, used in confined spaces, or employed without extensive martial training. They favour weapons suited to concealment, personal defence, hunting, and sudden violence rather than open warfare, and armour that preserves freedom of movement and careful control of body and equipment.

A thief may choose proficiency in non-exotic Civilian weapons. They may wear only leather, padded leather, studded leather, or elven chain armour, and do not receive training in the use of shields. Many of a thief's class abilities suffer penalties when wearing armour heavier than normal leather.

#### Thieving Skills

Thieves possess the full range of skills described in the Rogue Skills section of Chapter 10: Pick Pockets, Open Locks, Find and Remove Traps, Move Silently, Hide in Shadows, Detect Noise, Climb Walls, and (beginning at 4th level) Read Languages.

**Read Languages:** Unlike other rogue abilities, Read Languages cannot be used until the thief reaches **4th level**. Until then, the thief's listed percentage represents future aptitude and cannot be attempted, though discretionary points may still be allocated to it.

**Discretionary Point Spending:** Thieves may spend no more than half their discretionary points on a single rogue skill at each level.

#### Backstab

Though poor at prolonged toe-to-toe combat, thieves excel at striking unsuspecting opponents from advantageous positions.

When attacking from behind against a target unaware of the impending attack, the thief gains the normal rear attack benefits and inflicts additional damage according to Table 28: Backstab Damage Multipliers.

Only the first successful strike benefits from the multiplier. Once combat begins openly, the opportunity is lost.

Backstab functions only against creatures with vulnerable anatomies and identifiable weak points. Most humanoids are valid targets; many amorphous, gigantic, or anatomically alien creatures are not.

**Table 28: Backstab Damage Multipliers**

| **Thief's Level** | **Damage Multiplier** |
| ----------------- | --------------------- |
| 1-4               | ×2                    |
| 5-8               | ×3                    |
| 9-12              | ×4                    |
| 13+               | ×5                    |

#### Thieves’ Cant

All thieves learn a specialized criminal argot known as thieves’ cant.

Thieves’ cant is not a separate language, but a collection of slang, coded phrases, gestures, euphemisms, and layered meanings embedded within ordinary speech. It is used to identify fellow rogues, exchange warnings, discuss illicit business, and conceal meaning from outsiders.

Because it relies upon an existing spoken language, two thieves must still share a common tongue in order to communicate effectively.

#### Use Scrolls

At 10th level, a thief gains a limited ability to employ wizard and priest scrolls through practical familiarity with magical notation and ritual structure.

This understanding remains imperfect. Whenever a thief attempts to use a scroll, there is a 25% chance the spell is misread or improperly invoked, causing unintended or reversed effects as determined by the DM.

Such mishaps are rarely beneficial.

#### Strongholds and Followers

Thieves rarely construct obvious castles or fortresses. Instead, they prefer concealed headquarters, fortified safehouses, hidden compounds, or seemingly innocuous buildings concealing more secretive operations beneath.

A thief’s stronghold often reflects his profession and personality: a disguised warehouse, a maze-like manor filled with traps and hidden passages, a secluded lodge used by scouts and spies, or a covert urban network concealed behind legitimate businesses.

Upon reaching 10th level, a thief’s reputation attracts followers. These may be criminals, scouts, informants, apprentices, burglars, fences, or other rogues seeking protection, employment, or instruction.

The thief attracts 4d6 followers. Their exact nature may be determined by Table 31 - Thief's Followers or selected by the DM to suit the campaign.

Thieves are notoriously territorial. Rival gangs operating within the same region frequently come into violent conflict unless some arrangement or hierarchy can be established.

### Bard

**Ability Requirements:** Dex 12, Int 13, Cha 15

**Prime Requisites:** Dex, Cha

**Alignment:** Any Neutral

**Races Allowed:** Human, Half-elf

Bards live by wit, talent, charm, and adaptability. They are storytellers, musicians, heralds, wanderers, messengers, chroniclers, and negotiators, moving easily between courts, camps, taverns, and marketplaces. Inspired by the poets, skalds, troubadours, and oral historians of the real world, the bard in the game world preserves culture, carries news, immortalizes deeds, and navigates the shifting boundaries between social classes and peoples. Their required neutrality reflects the degree of detachment necessary to move among competing factions and survive in uncertain company.

The bard is the consummate generalist: capable enough in combat, skilled in social intrigue, and possessed of a small but versatile command of arcane magic.

All bards are accomplished performers. Every bard begins play proficient as a singer, chanter, poet, or storyteller, and also gains proficiency with one musical instrument of the player’s choice. These proficiencies are gained without cost. If the bard later spends proficiency slots on musical instruments, each slot grants proficiency with two instruments rather than one.

#### Arms and Armour

Whether performing in a noble court, entertaining in a crowded tavern, or travelling the open road as a wandering minstrel, bards encounter a wider variety of weapons and fighting traditions than most rogues. Though they are not trained as professional soldiers, experience, curiosity, and necessity often lead them to acquire a respectable degree of martial skill.

A bard may choose proficiency in non-exotic militia weapons. Bards may wear armour up to chain mail but are not trained in the use of shields. As with thieves, several bard abilities function best in lighter armour and may suffer penalties when heavier armour is worn.

#### Arcane Magic

During their travels, bards inevitably acquire fragments of magical learning: a half-remembered incantation from a hedge wizard, a song with hidden power, a forgotten charm copied from a noble’s library, or the secret refrain of an old ballad. Bardic magic is eclectic, opportunistic, and rarely systematic.

Bards cast arcane spells and maintain spell books much like wizards. Intelligence determines the number of spells a bard may learn and the chance to learn any particular spell. Bards follow the normal restrictions on memorization and spell use that apply to wizards, including the inability to cast arcane spells while wearing armour.

Unlike wizards, bards cannot specialize in a school of magic, nor do they automatically gain spells when advancing in level. The spells known at 2nd level are determined randomly or chosen by the DM, subject to normal spell learning checks. Additional spells must be discovered during play and copied into the bard’s spell book through study or adventure.

Because their magic is informal and pieced together through experience, bards often favour practical, entertaining, deceptive, or socially useful spells over purely destructive magic. Their caster level is equal to their bard level.

#### Bardic Talents

The bard's wandering life in pursuit of audiences, patrons, romance, lore, and adventure teaches him a handful of rogue skills. These are limited to **Detect Noise**, **Read Languages**, **Pick Pockets**, and **Climb Walls**, reflecting the needs of his profession rather than the specialized craft of the thief.

These skills are described in Chapter 10.

#### Influence and Inspiration

A bard can influence the reactions of listeners through performance, speech, poetry, music, humour, or storytelling. This ability functions only when the audience is not already engaged in immediate combat. Those listening must attempt a saving throw vs. paralyzation. The save is modified by −1 for every three levels of the bard.

Those who fail may have their reaction shifted one category toward either friendliness or hostility, at the bard’s choice. Those who succeed instead shift one category in the opposite direction. The performance must suit the audience and circumstances; a solemn dirge may calm frightened soldiers, while a rousing war song may inflame a mob.

Bards may also inspire allies before battle. If companions are aware of an impending conflict, the bard may spend three rounds performing to grant one of the following benefits to allies within 10 feet per level:

- +1 to attack rolls
    
- +1 to saving throws
    
- +2 morale

The inspiration lasts one round per level of the bard. Once expired, it cannot be renewed during the same battle, though troops withdrawn from combat may be inspired again later.

#### Counter Song

Bards can oppose magical effects conveyed through speech, music, chanting, poetry, or similar performance. By singing, reciting, or playing continuously, the bard grants allies within 30 feet protection against such attacks.

To maintain the counter song, the bard may take no action other than slow movement. If struck or forced to make a saving throw, the effort is disrupted. The bard must successfully save vs. spell for the counter song to function. If successful, the magical effect is negated for protected listeners; if failed, the effect resolves normally.

This ability functions against magically empowered speech, songs, compulsions, and similar effects, but not against ordinary verbal spell components or command words.

#### Lore and Knowledge

Bards gather scraps of knowledge everywhere they travel. All bards can read and write their native language if it possesses a written form, and all possess broad familiarity with the local history, legends, notable families, songs, battles, and scandals of their wider homeland.

In addition, bards have a 5% chance per level to identify the general nature of a magical item through examination and recollection of stories, songs, symbols, or historical references. Exact properties are not revealed, but the bard may discern whether an item is cursed, intelligent, legendary, associated with a notable wielder, or possessed of unusual significance.

#### Strongholds and Followers

At 9th level, a bard may establish a hall, theatre, manor, school, inn, courtly household, or similar stronghold. Upon doing so, the bard attracts 10d6 loyal 0-level soldiers, retainers, performers, students, or attendants over time. These followers are not automatically replaced if slain or dismissed.

At 10th level, the bard gains the ability to use magical writings normally restricted to other classes, including scrolls and certain magical books. However, bardic understanding of formal magic is imperfect. Whenever using such an item, there is a 15% chance the bard misreads or misinterprets it, causing the effect to function incorrectly or disastrously at the DM’s discretion.

### Ninja

**Ability Requirements:** Dex 13, Int 10

**Prime Requisites:** Dex

**Alignment:** Any Non-Chaotic

**Races Allowed:** Human, Dwarf, Gnome, Halfling

The ninja is a master of stealth, infiltration, and deception, trained from an early age in the arts of espionage, sabotage, and unconventional warfare. Although classed among the rogues, the ninja combines the subtle skills of the thief with broader martial training, allowing them to fight effectively while remaining specialists in secrecy and surprise. Some ninja traditions also preserve mystical disciplines or esoteric techniques represented by character kits.

Unlike ordinary adventurers, ninja are members of secret traditions whose loyalties often lie first with their clan or school. Their training emphasizes patience, discipline, careful planning, and the accomplishment of objectives with the least possible attention. They excel at reconnaissance, infiltration, ambush, intelligence gathering, and the elimination or capture of important targets. Although capable combatants, they rely upon surprise, preparation, and mobility rather than prolonged direct confrontation.

> **Ninja Races**
> 
> While demihumans of the indicated races are predisposed toward strong clan traditions _and_ may become ninjas, in most campaign settings ninja clans are a predominantly human phenomenon. Consequently, most non-human ninjas were raised from childhood within a human ninja clan.

#### Arms and Armour

Ninja are trained for infiltration, espionage, assassination, and covert warfare rather than open battle. Their martial instruction emphasizes concealment, deception, precision, and the effective use of specialized weapons intended to surprise an unprepared opponent or overcome unusual obstacles. Alongside the ordinary arms of travellers and civilians, ninja commonly learn uncommon or culturally distinctive weapons unfamiliar to most professional soldiers.

A ninja may choose proficiency in any civilian weapon, including exotic civilian weapons. In addition, they may choose proficiency in exotic militia weapons (but _not_ non-exotic ones).

Ninja may wear padded, leather, studded leather, hide, ring mail, scale mail, brigandine, or chain mail armour, and may employ shields. Although capable of wearing heavier armour, most ninja favour leather armour or lighter when undertaking missions requiring stealth, as many rogue abilities become less effective when heavier armour is worn.

#### Ninja Skills

Ninja possess the full range of rogue skills described in Chapter 10: Pick Pockets, Open Locks, Find and Remove Traps, Move Silently, Hide in Shadows, Detect Noise, Climb Walls, and Read Languages.

#### Backstab

A ninja may backstab under the same conditions and progression as described for thieves.

#### Clan Signs

Each ninja clan employs its own system of secret hand signs, written symbols, passwords, and other methods of covert communication. All members of the same clan readily understand these signs.

These signs are sufficient to communicate instructions and warnings, identify people, or convey other practical information, but are not suitable for lengthy or technical detail.

#### Followers

Upon attaining 10th level, a ninja becomes a leader within their clan and is assigned 2d6 followers, half are of the ninja class, while the remainder are other classes (all with the Shinobi character kit).

Unlike most other followers, their first loyalty is to the clan, and secondarily to the character.

Furthermore, the ninja is expected to develop his followers by planning the missions they will conduct in order to gain experience, and directly leading them when the risk is high.

A ninja who has followers die due to careless planning is likely to be punished by the Clan leader and may lose all followers. A ninja who sends his followers to certain death, or leaves them to it, dishonours his clan and is likely to face retribution.

### Monk (draft in progress)

**Ability Requirements:** Wisdom 15, Constitution 13

**Prime Requisites:** Wisdom, Constitution

**Races Allowed:** Human, Elf, Half-elf

Monks pursue perfection through the disciplined cultivation of body, mind, and spirit. Through rigorous training, meditation, physical conditioning, and philosophical study, they seek to transcend the limitations of ordinary mortals and achieve complete mastery of the self.

Unlike warriors, monks do not rely upon armour, battlefield formations, or martial strength alone. Their training develops balance, awareness, speed, resilience, and precise control of both body and mind. To a monk, combat is not separate from this discipline but one of its most demanding expressions: every movement, whether an attack, a defensive manoeuvre, or a step across the battlefield, is an opportunity to exercise control.

A monk's path is defined by a **Monastic Philosophy**. These philosophies represent systems of thought and practice through which monks interpret their training and pursue enlightenment. A philosophy may emphasize physical perfection, contemplation, self-denial, harmony, detachment, or any number of other ideals. Some may be associated with religious traditions, but a monk's philosophy is not a faith, and monks do not require divine patronage to pursue their path.

Monks combine exceptional physical ability with unusual mental and spiritual discipline. Their mastery develops gradually, and the abilities available to an experienced monk may appear almost supernatural to those who have not undergone similar training. Such feats are not granted by an external power: they are the result of years of disciplined practice and the cultivation of the monk's own potential.

#### Monastic Philosophy

Every monk belongs to a philosophical tradition that shapes his outlook, methods of training, and place within the world. A philosophy determines certain class features, behavioural expectations, and any additional restrictions or requirements beyond those of the monk class itself.

Monastic philosophies may differ considerably in their teachings. One tradition may emphasize detachment and stillness, another physical perfection and disciplined action, while another may teach harmony between opposing forces. These differences do not alter the fundamental nature of the monk's training, but they determine how that training is understood and applied.

A monk is expected to uphold the principles of his chosen philosophy. A serious and deliberate rejection of those principles may result in the loss of abilities or other consequences determined by the philosophy and the Dungeon Master.

#### Arms and Armour

Monastic traditions preserve many ancient and unusual martial disciplines long forgotten by conventional armies. Consequently, monks commonly study exotic weapons alongside more familiar arms, treating each as a means of cultivating discipline rather than instruments of war.

Monks devote themselves to the perfection of body, mind, and spirit through rigorous discipline and continual practice. Their training embraces both unarmed combat and the study of weapons as extensions of the self, each requiring balance, precision, and absolute control rather than brute force or battlefield doctrine.

A monk may choose proficiency in military weapons, including exotic weapons.

Monks do not receive training in the use of armour or shields. Their disciplines cultivate awareness, balance, and freedom of movement, all of which are hindered by protective equipment. Many monk abilities function only while unarmoured.

A monk may wear ordinary clothing, robes, or other garments that do not function as armour. Magical items that provide the benefits of armour or otherwise replace the protection normally provided by armour are generally prohibited to monks. Other magical items, including rings, cloaks, and similar objects that improve Armour Class without functioning as armour, may be used unless prohibited by the monk's philosophy.
